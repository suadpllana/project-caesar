`/app` is the layout half of a virtualized list pane, cut down to the part that decides where the pane scrolls to after each event and which rows it renders. A document is a run of groups. A group has a header of a declared height and rows whose real height the pane learns only by rendering them, so until then it works from the per-row estimate the group carries. `/app/run_pane.py` takes an event file and prints a line for each frame. `/app/evs` holds four event files.

The first line of an event file is `cfg V K P`, giving the viewport height, the overscan and the pass cap. Then come one or more `g <id> <header> <estimate> <low> <high> <rows>` lines, the groups in the order they stand in the document, with distinct ids. The rest are events, one frame each, in order. `scroll <d>` adds d to the offset and `go <p>` sets it to p. `size <v>` sets the viewport height to v. `ins <group> <at> <n>` puts n new rows into that group at that row position, and `del <group> <at> <n>` takes n rows out of that group from that row position. Every number is an integer, every height is at least 1, and every edit position and count lies inside its group.

The flow is, group by group, the header of a group and then its rows. Items are numbered from 0 across the whole flow and frames from 0. A header is as tall as the height its group declares. A row is as tall as the estimate its group carries until the pane has measured it, and as tall as its real height afterwards. A measured row stays measured for the rest of the run. The real height of a row is `low + (id * 2654435761) % (high - low + 1)`, over the bounds its group declares. Row ids are handed out from 0 in the order rows are created: the rows of each `g` line in turn, then the rows of each `ins` as it is replayed. The top of an item is the sum of the heights of the items before it, and the total is the sum of all of them. The foot is the total less the viewport height, or 0 when that would be negative. Clamping an offset means holding it at 0 below and at the foot above. The pane starts at offset 0.

The files you may change are `/app/pane/geom.py`, `/app/pane/band.py`, `/app/pane/win.py`, `/app/pane/hold.py`, `/app/pane/move.py` and `/app/pane/frame.py`. Nothing else. The rest of the tree is replaced by our own copy before a document is replayed, a new file put beside those six included.

The pinned group is the last group whose header top is at or before the offset. Its header is drawn across the top of the pane. The header of the next group pushes it off from below, so the band is the smaller of two numbers: the height of the pinned header, and the distance from the offset to the header top of the next group. For the last group the total stands in for that header top. The anchor line is at the offset plus the band.

An item is visible when its top is less than the offset plus the viewport height and its bottom is greater than the offset. The window runs from K items before the first visible item to K items after the last visible item, clipped to the ends of the flow. A pass measures, in item order, every row of its window that has not been measured yet.

The item a frame holds is the one whose top is at or before the anchor line and whose bottom is after it. When the line falls on the total it is the last item of the flow. The gap is the top of that item less the line. It is zero or negative.

A frame runs in this order. The movement the event asks for comes first: `scroll`, `go` and `size` change the offset or the viewport height and the offset is then clamped, while an insert or a delete moves nothing. The pane is following the foot when the offset that movement left behind is the foot. The hold is taken next, against the document as it stands before any insert or delete. Then the edit is applied. When it removes the held item, the hold becomes the first item that survives after it, or the last item that survives before it when none follows, and the gap moves by the difference between the top of the new item and the top of the old one as both stood before the edit.

The frame then settles, in at most P passes. A pass works out the band and the window from the offset it begins with and does its measuring. It then puts the offset at the foot if the pane is following the foot, and otherwise at the top of the held item, less the gap, less the band that pass worked out, clamped. A frame is settled once a pass has measured nothing and left the offset where it was. It stops after P passes however unsettled it is.

Each frame prints `f <frame> s <offset> g <group> b <band> w <first> <last> h <item> <gap> m <rows> p <passes>`. The offset is the one the frame ended at. The group is the id of the pinned group and the band is the band height, both as the last pass worked them out, and the window is the first and last item of the last pass. The item is the hold as it stood after the edit, written as `H` and the id of its group for a header and `R` and its row id for a row. The gap is the gap of that item. Both are printed whether or not the frame followed the foot instead of using them. `m` counts the rows this frame measured for the first time, over all its passes. `p` counts the passes it ran. After the last frame comes `end s <offset> t <total> m <rows>`, where the rows are every row the run measured, whether or not it is still in the document. Nothing else is printed.
