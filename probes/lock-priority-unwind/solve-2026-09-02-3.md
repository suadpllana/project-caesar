Rewritten `/app/rt/prio.py`.

**The bug:** the old policy pushed urgency *forward* only (lift the holder on `blocked`) and then unconditionally reset to base on *any* release (`released` → `set(t, base[t])`). So a task holding two mutexes lost its whole inheritance the moment it dropped the first one, even though a waiter was still queued behind the second. It also never handled `granted`/`expired` at all, and never propagated along chains.

**The new policy:** rather than tracking increments, each of the four hooks recomputes the invariant for every task from scratch off the core's live state:

> `eff[t] = max(base[t], max base among tasks queued — directly or through a chain of held mutexes — on anything t holds)`

Taking the max over *base* priorities across the whole transitive closure is equivalent to taking it over effective priorities (any task lifting a waiter is itself inside that closure), so there's no fixpoint iteration and no way to inherit a value nobody can account for. The traversal carries a visited set, so a mutex cycle (deadlock) terminates instead of spinning. Because every value is derived, not accumulated, a task drops to exactly the right level the instant its last relevant waiter leaves — whether it left by being granted the mutex, by the holder releasing, or by timing out.

**On your run** (`[5,4,5]`, `[7,4,9]`, `[10,4,1]` before), changes are now `[5,4,5]`, `[7,4,9]`, `[10,4,5]`, `[17,4,1]`: task 4 releases mutex 1 at tick 10 and falls only to 5, since task 2 is still queued on mutex 2 that it still holds. Task 1 finishes its section (11–13), task 4 then outranks task 3 and finishes its own (14–16), and task 2 is granted at tick 17 instead of 29 and completes at 21 instead of 33. Task 3 no longer runs ahead of the task that outranks it.

Verification, beyond that scenario:
- Chain case (t1@9 → t2@2 → t3@1 across two mutexes): both t2 and t3 lift to 9, and each drops at its own release, not the other's.
- Timeout case (t3@1 holds, t2@5 queued, t1@9 queued with a timeout): t3 goes 1→5→9, back to 5 the tick t1's wait expires, and to 1 at the release — the mutex and queue untouched by the expiry.
- Deadlock cycle: terminates, values correct.
- A 250-scenario randomized fuzz (2–5 tasks, 1–3 mutexes, nested sections, sleeps, mixed timeouts) checked against an independent implementation of the invariant that replays the event log to reconstruct holder/queue state per tick, rather than reusing the policy's own logic: zero mismatches in either direction (too low or too high).
