## Summary

Rewrote the four policy files under pol/ (adm.py, rtn.py, tear.py, emit.py) in place; lnk/, relay.py and cases/ are untouched. The shipped policy had three faults: it admitted against the figure just published (bk.pub) instead of the figure the producer had actually learned six ticks earlier (three ticks for the ceiling to reach it, three for the batch to come back), it never counted dropped or late rows as rows the link had finished with (so the link ceiling stayed at 120 for ever, which is the strand symptom), and it had no notion of a torn-down feed's gap, so nothing was ever 'late'. The new policy keeps, in the shared st dict (bootstrapped once by rtn.state from the book): a per-level publication history (tick, figure) that adm.verdict bisects to find the figure in force for a batch landing at tick W (latest figure published at a tick <= W-6, else the window: 40 for a feed's current run, 120 for the link); a link 'gone' counter fed by tear.shed (drops and late rows) so the link ceiling is 120 + drawn + gone; a per-run refused-rows counter per feed so the small-grant test uses what the producer believes it has sent; a set of levels touched during the tick; and a timer wheel of idle deadlines (last accepted tick + 7) so emit.plan only evaluates levels that changed this tick or whose idle clock runs out on it, which is what keeps a 10,000-feed / 160,000-tick stream at about one second instead of scanning every open feed every tick. adm.verdict: a down feed's arrival is 'late' while when < shut_tick + 6 and the link's learned figure is not exceeded, 'over' otherwise; a live feed's arrival is 'over' if the feed's count or the link's count would pass its learned figure (refused rows are added to the feed's believed count), else 'ok' (feed idle deadline rescheduled). emit.plan: a feed's ceiling is drawn + 40, or drawn + 12 once idle for seven ticks; the link's is 120 + drawn + gone; a grant is emitted only if the value is above the standing (last published, in flight included) figure and either the raise is >= 20 or the standing figure leaves the producer's believed count under 5 rows while the new one gives it at least 5; a pull is emitted only for an idle feed whose floor ceiling is below the standing figure. tear.opened resets a feed's history and refused count and starts its idle clock. Verified on the three shipped streams (hand-traced tick by tick and matching) plus four scratch streams exercising teardown gaps, late-vs-over at the link, reopen in the same tick as an arrival in both orders, and a wide stream (10k feeds, 160k ticks, 117k events, 1.0 s wall); scratch files were removed afterwards.

## Rules as read

- A ceiling is an absolute running total: window for that level (40 feed / 120 link) plus the rows that level has finished with. For a feed that is rows drawn from the current run; for the link it is rows drawn plus rows dropped at teardown plus late rows thrown away, over the whole stream.
- A producer goes by a figure published at the end of tick T from tick T+3, and a batch landing at tick W left at W-3, so a batch landing at W is judged against the latest figure published at a tick <= W-6; with nothing published yet to that level (or to that run of the feed) it goes by the window.
- An arrival on a live feed is refused ('over') if feed count + rows exceeds the feed's learned figure or link count + rows exceeds the link's learned figure; either alone suffices; nothing moves. Otherwise it is 'ok' and parked.
- A grant is published when the new ceiling is above the standing figure and either the raise is >= 20, or the standing figure (in-flight figures count as standing) would leave the producer unable to send 5 rows while the new ceiling would allow >= 5. Nothing else earns a grant, and a figure is never published unless it is above the standing one, except the idle pull.
- The small-grant test uses what the producer believes it has sent: accepted rows plus rows we refused from that feed in its current run, because a refused batch is sent as far as its producer knows.
- A feed with nothing accepted for seven ticks (when - last accepted >= 7, the arm tick counting as the clock start) falls back to a ceiling of drawn + 12; if that is below the standing figure it is published as a 'pull'. The floor window stays in force until something is accepted again.
- Teardown throws away parked rows as one 'drop' with the total; a teardown of a feed already down does nothing; a draw against a feed holding nothing, or a feed that is gone, does nothing.
- After a teardown at tick X, the producer keeps sending until it learns at X+3, so batches from that feed landing at ticks X..X+5 are 'late' (charged to the link only, thrown away, counted as finished by the link) unless they would carry the link past its learned figure, in which case they are 'over'; from X+6 every arrival on that feed is 'over'.
- A reopened feed is a fresh run: counts start from nothing, the new producer knows the 40-row window at once (no lag), everything published to the old run is forgotten, the idle clock restarts at the reopen tick, and the link's figure is learned with the usual lag like everyone else. The link never restarts.
- Events inside one tick run in stream order; figures are published once at the end of the tick, at most one per level, and the machine sorts them.
- The whole graded run must stay well inside ten minutes on two processors even with ~10k feeds over ~150k ticks, so the publish step must not scan every open feed every tick.

## Judgment calls

- Round-trip lag: I read 'goes by it from the third tick after it' as tick T+3 inclusive, giving a batch landing at W the latest figure published at a tick <= W-6. The alternative (T+4) would shift every admission decision by a tick.
- The small-grant ('owed') test for a feed compares the standing figure against the producer's believed count (accepted + refused rows this run), not our own charged count, because the brief says a refused batch is still sent as far as its producer knows. For the link I used our own count (lsnt), since no producer can see what the others have sent and there is no single producer to believe anything.
- The idle clock runs from the arm tick (start of stream or reopen), not only from the first accepted batch: the book sets last=t on arm, and a feed with nothing accepted for seven ticks since it opened literally matches the rule. Consequence: every feed silent since tick 0 is pulled to 12 at tick 7 (strand shows this for feed 3).
- Idle boundary: 'for seven ticks' taken as when - last >= 7 evaluated at publish time, as the shipped tear.window had it, treating the arm tick like an acceptance tick.
- The end of the teardown gap is time-based: late is possible for landings at ticks X..X+5 (the last batch the old producer could send leaves at X+2 and lands at X+5), and everything from X+6 on is over, since which batch was actually the last cannot be known.
- When a feed is idle and a draw raises its floor ceiling above the standing figure, that counts as an ordinary raise subject to the 20-row threshold and the small-grant test, still using the 12-row floor window until something is accepted; a pull is only ever emitted when the floor ceiling is below the standing figure.
- Refused rows are added to the producer's believed count regardless of whether the refusal was for the feed limit or the link limit; refused rows on a torn-down feed are not tracked (that run is gone). Late rows are not added to any feed count.
- The standing figure for both the never-below-standing rule and the small-grant test is bk.pub (the last figure published, in flight or not), per 'a figure still on its way counts as published for that test'.
- For a feed whose pub entry was popped by teardown, nothing is ever published; for a feed reopened and torn down in the same tick (not in graded streams) the code simply skips it.
- Scheduling: emit.plan evaluates only levels touched during the tick plus feeds whose idle deadline (last accepted + 7) falls on that tick; evaluation is a pure function of the book state so extra evaluations are harmless, and a stale deadline is skipped when the feed has since accepted something or gone down.
