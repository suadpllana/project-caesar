## Summary

Rewrote the four policy files in pol/ (adm.py, rtn.py, tear.py, emit.py). Nothing under lnk/, relay.py or cases/ was touched; the scratch files and bytecode caches created while testing were removed, so the tree holds exactly its original twelve files.

What was wrong and what changed:
- pol/adm.py: admission was graded against the figure the relay had just published (bk.pub), not the one the producer had learned. verdict() now keeps a per-level publication history in st and takes the latest figure published at a tick T with T+3 <= now, falling back to the window (40 for a feed, 120 for the link). An arrival on a closed feed is late during the teardown tick and the two after it (link test only, against the learned link figure) and over after that; an unknown feed is over. Every refused batch on a live feed is added to a per-feed refused count, because its producer still counts it as sent. Any arrival on a live feed marks that feed as touched for the tick's publishing pass.
- pol/rtn.py: the link's finished-with count ignored rows that were dropped or thrown away late, so after the three teardowns in strand.json the link ceiling stayed at 120 and feed 3 was refused for ever. drained() now reads the book's own draw totals and adds the link's lost rows; ceiling() is drained plus the window (idle floor or 40 for a feed, 120 for the link). took() marks the feed touched.
- pol/tear.py: shed() accumulates every dropped or late row into one link-level lost total; opened() forgets the old run's publication history and refused count and touches the feed; due() arms a feed's idle deadline at last-accepted-tick + 7 (deduplicated per feed); window() returns the floor of 12 once 7 ticks have passed with nothing accepted.
- pol/emit.py: plan() no longer walks every open feed every tick. It grades the link each tick plus only the feeds touched this tick (ok arrival, refusal, draw, reopen) or whose idle deadline falls on this tick, re-arming the deadline of every touched feed; on the first tick it arms every feed's deadline once from the book. A grant needs value above the standing (last published, in flight or not) figure and either a raise of 20+ or the small-grant test; the small-grant test uses accepted + refused rows for a feed and the relay's own lsnt for the link. Anything below the standing figure (only the idle floor can do that) is a pull. Every publication is appended to the history adm.py reads.

## Rules as read

- A ceiling is an absolute running total: the level's window (40 feed, 120 link) laid on top of the rows that level has finished with. For a feed that is the rows drawn from it this run; for the link it is rows drawn plus rows dropped at a teardown plus rows thrown away as late, because none of those may still hold link permit once it has left us.
- A producer acts on the latest figure published at a tick T with T + 3 <= the current tick; before any such figure exists it goes by the window. A reopened feed goes by 40 at once and forgets everything published to the old run. The link never restarts.
- Admission: a batch on a live feed is refused (over) if feed-sent + rows exceeds the learned feed figure or link-sent + rows exceeds the learned link figure; either level alone refuses it and nothing moves. Otherwise it is ok and parked.
- A batch on a feed torn down at tick S, arriving at S, S+1 or S+2, is late (charged to the link, thrown away) unless it would carry the link past the learned link figure, in which case it is over. From S+3 on every batch from that feed is over.
- A grant is published at the end of a tick when the new ceiling is above the standing figure (the last one published, whether or not it has arrived) and either the raise is 20 or more, or the standing figure would leave the producer unable to send 5 rows while the new one would let it. Nothing else earns a grant.
- The producer's own sent count used by the small-grant test is, for a feed, the rows accepted this run plus every batch refused this run (a refused batch is still sent as far as its producer knows); for the link it is the relay's own count lsnt (accepted plus late rows, never refused ones).
- A feed with nothing accepted for 7 ticks (current tick minus the tick of its last accepted batch, or its arm/reopen tick, at least 7) has its window replaced by the floor of 12; if that puts the ceiling below the standing figure it is published as a pull. A raise while idle is still a grant under the normal tests.
- A teardown throws parked rows away as one drop; a draw on an empty or gone feed, a teardown of a gone feed and a reopen of a live feed are all quiet. Events run in stream order within a tick and publishing happens once the tick is over, at most one figure per level per tick.
- The graded run of many streams, some with ~10000 feeds over ~150000 ticks, has ten minutes on two processors, so the publishing pass must be driven by what changed in the tick (plus idle deadlines) rather than by walking every open feed.

## Judgment calls

- Idle threshold boundary: I read 'nothing accepted for seven ticks' as (tick - last) >= 7, where last is the book's own bk.last (tick of the last accepted batch, or the arm/reopen tick), so a feed armed at tick 0 with nothing accepted is pulled to 12 at tick 7 and a feed last fed at tick 8 is pulled at tick 15. The alternative (> 7, or counting the arm tick itself) would shift every pull by one tick.
- Feeds never touched by the stream are still pulled to the floor at arm + 7: I applied the idle rule literally to every feed the stream declares, including the thousands a wide stream never uses, since the brief states the rule with no exception.
- Learned-figure timing: a figure published at the end of tick T is what the producer goes by from tick T + 3 inclusive ('from the third tick after it'), so an arrival at tick 16 sees a figure published at tick 13.
- Late-window arrivals are tested only against the link (the learned link figure), never against any feed figure, since the feed is gone and its permit is not charged.
- The refused count feeding the small-grant test includes batches refused for the link's sake as well as for the feed's, and is reset on reopen; batches refused after a teardown (late window or later) are not counted anywhere, since that run is over and a reopen starts from nothing.
- The small-grant test compares against the standing (last published, possibly in flight) figure, and the threshold raise is likewise measured from that standing figure, per 'a figure still on its way counts as published for that test' and 'never publish a figure that is not above the one already standing'.
- The link's finished-with count includes late rows: they are billed to the link and thrown away, so they have left us and may not hold permit. This means a late batch raises the link ceiling by its own size in the same tick.
- Pulls are emitted whenever the ceiling falls below the standing figure, which only the idle floor can cause; a pull is published once (the next tick the value equals the new standing figure) and an idle feed that is drawn from gets a normal grant if the raise clears the threshold or the small-grant test.
- Reopen in the same tick as a teardown, or an arrival in the same tick as either, follows stream order as the machine applies it: x then a is late, o then a is a fresh-run arrival against 40, a then x is dropped.
- An arrival on a feed the stream never declared is refused as over (defensive; the book has no shut tick for it), and a 0-row batch is treated like any other batch.
- For draw totals I read bk.tkn/bk.ltkn straight off the frozen book rather than keeping a parallel tally in st, so the policy cannot drift from the machine's own count; st holds only what the book does not: publication history per level, refused rows per feed, the link's lost rows, the touched set and the idle deadlines.
- Verification without an answer key: I trusted agreement between the policy, an independent model written from the brief, and a naive all-feeds-every-tick plan over 6000 random streams, plus hand traces, as the evidence that the reading is self-consistent and the schedule loses nothing; that cannot rule out a reading of the brief the grader makes differently.
