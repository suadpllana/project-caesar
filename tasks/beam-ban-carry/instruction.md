`/app` is the search stage of a decoding server, cut down to the part that decides which sequences a request gets back. A program file carries the settings, a scoring table over token pairs, and the requests to run. `/app/run_beam.py` takes one and prints what each request did. `/app/asks` holds four programs. `cfg W N S P H T` sets the beam width, the span length, the floor a hypothesis has to reach, the length penalty, the size of the kept set and the step ceiling. `sc a b n` says the search may take token b from token a and add n to the score. There is at most one row for a pair, and every score is a whole number, zero or more. `ask name t ...` is one request and the prompt it starts from. Token 0 is the stop token and never appears in a prompt. N is at least 2 and S at least 1. The width, the ceiling and the size of the kept set are never below 1.

The files you may change are `/app/bm/sc.py`, `/app/bm/rep.py`, `/app/bm/keep.py`, `/app/bm/pick.py`, `/app/bm/walk.py` and `/app/bm/halt.py`. Nothing else. The rest of the tree is replaced by our own copy before a program is run, a new file put beside those six included, and `one(sp, name, prompt)` in `/app/bm/walk.py` stays the entry the driver calls once per request, returning the lines that request prints.

A request begins with a single beam holding the prompt, a score of zero and nothing emitted. The sequence a beam stands on is the prompt followed by what it has emitted. A span is the last N tokens of a sequence, so a sequence of fewer than N tokens has none. Steps are numbered from 1. The beams of a step stand in an order, and that order decides every tie below.

A beam closes when it has emitted at least S tokens and the table has a row taking the stop token from the last token of its sequence. Closing leaves it standing, and the score of that beam does not change. The hypothesis it makes holds the tokens that beam emitted, its length is how many those are, and its final score is the score of the beam plus the score on that stop row, less P times that length. Every beam that can close does so, in the order the beams stand. That happens before any candidate of the step is looked at.

At most H hypotheses are kept. One of them stands above another when its final score is higher, or when the two scores are equal and it is the shorter, or when score and length are both equal and it closed earlier. Each hypothesis is taken in as it closes. While more than H are held, the one standing last is given up.

Every member of the kept set lends the search each span of its sequence, prompt included, for as long as it is a member. A candidate is refused if the span it would add is one the set is lending. It is refused as well when that span already occurs in the beam's own sequence. A member that is given up takes its lending with it. A span some other member is still lending stays refused.

A beam offers one candidate for each token the table has a row for from the last token of its sequence. The stop token is not one of them. Such a candidate scores the score of the beam plus the score on the row. The candidates that were not refused are ordered by score descending. A tie goes to the candidate whose beam stands earlier, and then to the smaller token. They are taken down that order, passing over one whose token a candidate already taken ends on, until W have been taken or the order runs out. Those become the beams of the next step, in the order they were taken.

The search then decides whether to go on. It halts `dry` when nothing was taken. It halts `cap` when the step just run is T. It halts `bound` when the kept set holds H and no beam has a reach greater than the lowest final score in it. Those three are read in that order, and the search continues when none of them holds.

The reach of a beam starts at its score, less P times the number of steps run so far. To that add the steps it could still run, which is T less the step just run, times the largest score anywhere in the table less P. Count that difference as zero when it comes out negative.

A request prints `ask` and its name. Then come `shut <step> <length> <final>` as each hypothesis closes and `gone <step> <length> <final>` as each one is given up, in the order those happen, and after them `halt <step> <reason>`. Last is the kept set as it finally stands, one `hyp <rank> <final> <length> <token> ...` to a line, the one standing highest first. Ranks count from 0. Requests are run in the order they appear. Each starts from nothing: no beam, no member and no lending carried over from the one before. Nothing else is printed.

`/app/asks/long.txt` is five requests of two thousand eight hundred steps over eight beams. `/app/asks/turn.txt` is five of nine hundred whose kept set of thirty turns over on nearly every step.
