`/app` is the sample feed of a training run, cut down to the part that decides which sample reaches which rank. A source is a corpus: `src <name> <n> <w> <cap>` declares one holding `n` samples numbered from 0, weighing `w`, and allowed `cap` epochs, where a cap of 0 is no limit; it starts at epoch 0 with its cursor at 0. The live sources and their weights are the blend, and the blend belongs to the manifest, which outlives anything that happens to the run. `run <ranks> <micro> <accum>` starts the trainer at that many ranks, that micro-batch size and that many accumulation steps, `go <k>` takes `k` optimizer steps, `save` writes a checkpoint, `stop` is the trainer dying, `wt <name> <w>` reweighs a source, `seed <s>` fixes the seed the permutations come from, and of the two questions `feed <rank> <slot>` asks which samples that rank would be handed in that slot of the step about to be taken while `at <name>` asks how far a source has got. A script is a text file of those lines and `/app/progs` holds four of them. `/app/run_mix.py` takes a script and prints a line for each thing that happens.

A step takes `ranks * micro * accum` draws and each draw is one sample. A draw goes to the live source whose counter divided by its weight is smallest, and when two of them are equal the source declared earlier takes it; the draw then adds one to that source's counter and to no other. A counter counts draws taken since the blend last changed rather than since the run began. Declaring a source, reweighing one and a source leaving are each a change, and each puts every live source's counter back to 0 before the next draw is made - a `wt` line included, whatever weight it names, the one already in force included.

Each source serves its samples in a permutation of 0 to `n - 1` settled by the seed, the source's place in the declaration order and the epoch, which `/app/mix/perm.py` already computes. A draw takes the sample under the source's cursor and moves the cursor on by one, and a cursor reaching `n` starts the next epoch with the cursor back at 0. Rather than start the epoch its cap numbers, a capped source leaves the blend: it goes at the draw that takes the last sample of epoch `cap - 1`, and it goes before any further draw, so the rest of that step is drawn from a blend without it.

Draw `i` of a step, counting from 0, belongs to rank `(i // micro) % ranks` in slot `i // (micro * ranks)`, and takes place `i % micro` of that micro-batch. Which source a draw goes to is settled before any of that, and does not depend on the rank count, the micro-batch size or the accumulation depth.

Steps are numbered from 0 across the whole script. A checkpoint holds the step number, and the epoch, cursor and counter of every source declared by the time it is written. A `stop` puts those back and nothing else: who is live and what they weigh is the manifest's, so a source that has left stays out, a weight changed since stays changed, and a source declared after the checkpoint was written keeps the epoch and cursor it has reached. Counters written down under one blend are worth having only under that blend, so the ones a `stop` puts back stand when the live sources and their weights are what they were when the checkpoint was written, and every live source's counter is 0 when they are not. A `stop` always follows a `save`, and a `go` or a `feed` always follows a `run`.

A `feed` is a question about a step that has not been taken. It reports that micro-batch as it would be, a departure inside the step included, and leaves the run exactly where it found it, so two `feed` lines running report the same samples and a departure a `feed` saw is not announced. `at` reports the epoch and cursor a source stands at.

Every line is printed at the moment the thing happens. A source leaving prints `done <name> <step> <draw>`, naming the step holding the draw that took its last sample and that draw's number inside the step counting from 0. The two questions print `feed <rank> <slot> <name>:<sample> ...`, one entry per place in the micro-batch in the order the draws are taken, or `at <name> <epoch> <cursor>`, or `at <name> out` where the source has left. Nothing else is printed.

A script declares at most six sources, the first of them always uncapped; `n` is at most 65536, `w` at most 64 and a cap at most 2000000. `ranks` is at most 16, `micro` at most 8, `accum` at most 32, and `ranks * micro * accum` at most 2048; a `feed` names a rank below `ranks` and a slot below `accum`. A single `go` takes at most four million steps. `/app/progs/wide.txt` takes three million one hundred thousand steps across a restart that changes all three widths, a little over two thousand million draws, with a source leaving on each side of that restart.
