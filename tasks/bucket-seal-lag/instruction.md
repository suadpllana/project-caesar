The tree under `/app` is a small stage machine we use to try routing ideas out before they go anywhere near the real pipeline. A plan is a text file of nodes, the wires between them and a script of things a source does; `/app/plans` holds three, and `/app/run_flow.py` takes one and prints what happened, a row to a line. Run it on `/app/plans/relay.txt` and the last thing that happens to bucket 2 is `["ls", 6, "g", 2, 26]`, an item turning up for a bucket shut against it two ticks earlier at `["sl", 4, "g", 2, [22]]`, while the source that sent it was open and standing on a floor of 20. That row should not exist.

Here is the machine it came out of. Items carry an integer stamp and nothing else. A wire adds its lag to the stamp of whatever crosses it. A `relay` hands what it takes to every wire leaving it, a `lift` raises anything beneath its own floor to that floor and passes it along, and a `gather` of width `w` drops each item into the bucket its stamp falls in and keeps that bucket open; when the bucket is sealed the gather emits one item carrying the highest stamp that bucket covers, and anything reaching a bucket already sealed is lost where it lands. A `sink` keeps what it is given. A source releases items, announces a floor it will never go beneath again, and eventually shuts. Whatever ends up at or past the horizon named in the plan is discarded instead of delivered. One item moves per tick, at the earliest node in name order holding any, and seals falling on a single tick are written in order of gather name and then bucket. Every one of those events is a row: releases, announcements, the shut, an opening, a joining, a sealing with its members listed, a loss, a receipt at a sink, a discard, and the tick everything finished on.

Sealing is the decision we rewrote last cycle, and it has been wrong ever since. `/app/plans/direct.txt` still behaves, because there the source feeds the gather with no relay between them: bucket 0 goes at `["sl", 3, "g", 0, [5]]` as the floor climbs to 20, and bucket 2 at `["sl", 6, "g", 2, [22, 26]]` as it climbs to 40. `/app/plans/relay.txt` is that same script through one relay, and you saw where it ended. `/app/plans/redrive.txt` carries a wire from the gather back into the pipeline and fails alike, at `["sl", 6, "g", 4, [40, 40]]`.

We need the machine sealing on time again. A bucket is sealed at the first tick at which every stamp still able to reach its gather lies above the highest stamp that bucket covers, and not before. We grade both halves. Keeping a bucket open once nothing at or beneath it can arrive any more is wrong. So is shutting one while something yet can, and that is what produced the loss above. Reckon from where matters stand at the moment you are asked, never from what a plan turns out to do afterwards. An open source may release anything at or above the floor it last announced, as often as it pleases; once shut, it is finished. Anything else in the machine can pass along whatever it holds, and what becomes of a stamp travelling somewhere is whatever the nodes and wires between here and there do to it.

The plans we grade are no straight lines, and four of their shapes are worth spelling out, since a rule fitted to a straight line survives none. Wires double back to nodes an item has already crossed. One such wire can return to the very gather that emitted an item, so what a gather keeps today may reach that same gather again later and higher. A lift stands on some routes into a gather, a gather stands on some routes into another gather, and sources linger open on a low floor while buckets fill above them.

Four behaviours must survive, and we grade those as hard as the rest. A plan holding no way back and no lift seals each bucket on the tick its floor passes. A source that has shut keeps nothing waiting behind it. A gather wired directly to a source seals on the floor that source announced plus the lag of that single wire, with nothing else to weigh. And a lift whose floor lies beneath everything in the plan alters nothing whatever.
