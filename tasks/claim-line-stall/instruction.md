`/app` is the claim service of the storage engine, cut down to the part that decides what happens to an ask. Jobs take claims on units and on the cells inside them. A scope is either a unit, written `u3`, or one of its cells, written `u3/c5`, and every claim is held in one of two modes, `r` or `w`. A program is a text file of ops, one to a line, and `/app/progs` holds four of them. `take j2 u3/c5 w` asks for a claim. `drop j2 u3/c5` gives up the claim that job holds on exactly that scope, if it holds one. `end j2` gives up everything it holds, and `show u3` reports on a unit. `/app/run_hold.py` takes a program and prints a line for each thing that happens.

The last line comes out `grant j3 u1/c1 r` and it should read `wait j3 u1/c1 r`, since j1 still holds that cell for writing and asking again for something you already hold does not weaken it. The files you may change are `/app/hold/book.py`, `/app/hold/line.py`, `/app/hold/lift.py`, `/app/hold/knot.py`, `/app/hold/turn.py` and `/app/hold/act.py`. Nothing else.

A unit covers itself and every cell of it. A cell covers only itself. Two scopes overlap when either covers the other, and claims held by two different jobs conflict when their scopes overlap and at least one of the two is `w`. An ask conflicts with a claim, or with an ask of another job, by that same test, and the claims a job holds never conflict with an ask of its own. An ask that a claim the job already holds covers, in a mode at least as strong, is granted and changes nothing at all. Any other ask is granted when no claim of another job conflicts with it and no ask of another job that conflicts with it stands ahead of it. It joins the line when either of those does. An ask stands ahead of another when at that moment its job holds a claim overlapping what it asked for and the other job does not, and two asks on the same side of that stand in the order they were filed.

Four cell claims already held in one unit turn the next ask for a cell of that unit into an ask for the whole unit. Its mode is the strongest among the one asked for, the modes of those four or more cell claims, and any claim the job holds on the unit itself. Nothing is given up for that ask until it is granted. A granted claim then takes the place of every claim the job holds on a scope it strictly covers. A job that already has an ask waiting cannot ask for anything, so a `take` naming it does nothing and prints nothing, while `drop` and `end` still work.

After an op that releases a claim or discards an ask, the service goes once down the line in order and grants every ask that can be granted at the point it is reached. It does not go back.

A job is held up by another when an ask of its own is refused because that other job holds a conflicting claim, or because that other job has a conflicting ask of its own standing ahead of it in the line, and those two are the whole of it. When following held-up-by out of a job can come back to that job, none of the jobs on that loop can ever proceed. The service looks for that after every op, and cancels exactly one of them: of every job that can be reached from itself this way it takes the one holding fewest claims, and of those the one that started most recently, releasing the claims of that job and discarding its ask. It then settles the line and looks again. A job starts when it comes to hold a claim or to have an ask waiting while it had neither. It is forgotten once it holds nothing and waits for nothing. A job that appears again after that has started again.

Every line is printed when it happens. An ask prints `grant <job> <scope> <mode>` when it is granted and `wait <job> <scope> <mode>` when it joins the line, with the scope and mode as they stand after any raising. A drop prints `free <job> <scope> <claims the job holds afterwards>` whether or not it found a claim to release. `end` prints `end <job> <claims released>`. A cancelled job prints `stop <job> <claims released>`. `show <unit>` prints `show <unit> <claims> <waiting>`, where `<claims>` is every granted claim whose scope overlaps that unit, written as `<job>@<scope>=<mode>` and joined by commas, ordered by when the job started, with the unit itself ahead of its cells and cells by number, or `-` when there are none, and where `<waiting>` is how many asks whose scope overlaps that unit are in the line. Nothing else is printed.

`/app/progs/wide.txt` holds sixteen hundred units, stands three thousand asks on them and then runs twenty-six thousand takes and as many drops beside that line. `/app/progs/tall.txt` gathers cell claims until asks are raised to whole units, fifteen hundred times over, while two thousand asks stand on other units.
