`/app` is the resolver of our settings service, cut down to the part that turns a journal of operator changes into the board the rest of the estate reads. A program file is one such journal with the questions put to it, and `/app/run_conf.py` takes one and prints a line per answer. Sections and names are whole numbers, never negative. A slot is a section and a name together, and it holds a number, or stands masked, or is empty. A section may link to one other section. `/app/progs` holds four programs.

An entry line makes one change. `sec s` moves the journal into section s, `set n v` puts v in the slot for name n, `clr n` empties that slot, `cut n` masks it, `add n d` steps it by d, and `lnk p` links the section the journal is in to p. Any entry may be written behind `if g w` or behind `once g w`. Entry lines are numbered from 0 in the order they appear; `open`, `shut`, `off`, `back`, `get` and `all` are not entries and take no number. A change is what `open` and `shut` bracket, and an entry outside a bracket is a change of its own; changes are numbered from 0 in the order they are opened, counting a bracket and a bare entry alike. Then `off c` withdraws change c and `back c` puts it back. `get s n` asks for name n in section s. `all` asks for the whole board. A value, a step and the number a condition asks for may be negative.

The files you may change are `/app/cf/book.py`, `/app/cf/sect.py`, `/app/cf/step.py`, `/app/cf/gate.py`, `/app/cf/wake.py`, `/app/cf/walk.py` and `/app/cf/tell.py`. Nothing else. The rest of the tree is replaced by our own copy before a program is run, a new file put beside those seven included.

Reading a name in a section takes the number the slot for it there holds. A masked slot ends the reading with nothing found. An empty one sends the reading on to the section this one links to and the same rule applies there. A reading that comes to a section linking nowhere ends with nothing found. So does one that comes to a section it has already been at.

A question is answered by settling the board from nothing, over the entries that stand above that question in the file. A pass begins from nothing: every slot empty, no section linked, the journal in section 0. It walks those entries in number order. Each is applied in the section the walk is in when it is reached, so `set`, `clr` and `cut` work the slot there. `add n d` reads n from that section by the rule above and, when the reading finds a number, puts that number and d together in the slot for n there. When the reading finds nothing the entry does nothing. An entry written behind `if g w` is applied only when reading g from that section finds exactly w, and otherwise does nothing at all, a `sec` or `lnk` entry included.

An entry written behind `once g w` is asleep, and every settle starts with all of them asleep however an earlier one ended. Once a pass is over, the sleeping entry with the lowest number whose reading of g finds exactly w wakes and another pass runs. That reading is taken in the section the walk was in at the place of that entry during the pass just finished, over the board that pass finished with. An entry that has woken is applied by the passes after it and is not read for again. The settle is over once no reading of a sleeping entry finds its number. Passes are counted from one.

The entries of a withdrawn change take no part in any pass. They write nothing and move the journal nowhere. None of them can wake. Withdrawing a change already withdrawn changes nothing, and so does putting back one that already stands.

`get s n` prints `get <section> <name> <value> <passes>`, with `-` in place of the value when the reading finds nothing. `all` prints `all <passes> <held> <masked> <linked>`, where held counts the slots holding a number, masked the slots standing masked and linked the sections linking somewhere. Under that line come `v <section> <name> <value>` for every slot holding a number, then `m <section> <name>` for every masked slot. Last go `l <section> <target>` for every section that links somewhere. Each of the three runs goes in order of section and then of name. The pass count in either line is the one from the settle that answered that question. Nothing else is printed.

`/app/progs/wide.txt` puts some eleven thousand questions to a journal of some fifteen thousand entries, all of it in one section. `/app/progs/deep.txt` puts some fifteen thousand to a journal of some twenty-two thousand, over sections linked six deep.
