`/app` is the dispatch stage of a sparse layer, cut down to the part that decides which expert buffers a token enters. A step file gives the gate scores of every token of an optimizer step, microbatch by microbatch. `/app/run_lay.py` takes one and prints a line for each thing that happens. `cfg E B W F G` opens the file and sets the number of experts, the bank width, the want threshold, the capacity factor as a percentage and the bank share as a percentage; `step` begins a step, `mb` begins a microbatch inside it, and `t` gives one token its E gate scores as non-negative integers. Experts are numbered from 0. Bank k holds the B experts from kB. Tokens are numbered from 0 inside each step, in the order they appear. `/app/plans` holds four step files.

We rewrote this half last cycle and it has been wrong since. Run `/app/run_lay.py` on `/app/plans/tiny.txt`. The line for token 2 comes out `tok 2 res 1000`, and it should read `tok 2 0:1 res 0`: expert 0 was full, token 2 outscores the token sitting in slot 1 of it, and a token that got everything it wanted has nothing left over.

The files you may change are `/app/lay/gate.py`, `/app/lay/cap.py`, `/app/lay/buf.py`, `/app/lay/back.py`, `/app/lay/put.py`, `/app/lay/trim.py` and `/app/lay/tally.py`. Nothing else. The rest of the tree is replaced by our own copy before a program is run, a new file put beside those seven included.

A token ranks the experts by gate score descending, a tie going to the smaller expert index. It wants the shortest run of that ranking from the top whose scores add up to W or more, and the whole ranking when the whole ranking adds up to less than W. Buffers are allocated once per step, before it runs. Every expert gets C slots numbered from 0, where C is F times the number of expert places the tokens of the first microbatch of the step want between them, times the number of microbatches in the step, over a hundred times the number of experts, rounded up. A bank may hold Z places when the step is over. Z is G times C times the bank width over a hundred, rounded up.

Inside a microbatch the tokens queued by the microbatch before it go first, in the order they were queued, and then its own in token order. A token takes the experts it wants in rank order, at the lowest free slot of each, and stops at the first one it cannot enter, so what it holds is always a run of its want list from the top. An expert with no free slot is not closed. Among the occupants that have not already been displaced in this step, the one with the lowest gate score at that expert, a tie going to the larger slot index, gives up its place when the arriving token scores strictly higher there. The arrival takes that exact slot. An occupant that gives up a place loses every place it holds further down its own want list as well, and those slots are free from then on. A token that loses or never gets the first expert of its want list holds nothing and is queued for the next microbatch, printing `def` and its number. There is no next microbatch after the last one. A token losing the first expert there is left with nothing and prints nothing. A token that keeps the first expert is never queued, whatever it lost after it. An expert that turned a token away, by being full with nobody in it that could be displaced or by taking its place, is struck out of the ranking of that token for the rest of the step. Its want list is worked out again over what is left, by the same rule, every time it is looked at.

When the last microbatch is done the banks are gone over in index order. While a bank holds more than Z places, the lowest-scoring place in it goes, a tie going to the larger expert index and then to the larger slot index, and with it every place its token holds further down the want list of that token, wherever those sit. Slots freed this way are not filled again. Each step starts over: empty buffers, an empty queue, nothing struck out and nobody marked as displaced.

`cap C Z` is printed as a step begins, and `def` with the number of a token when that token is queued. Once the banks are done every token of the step prints, in token order, `tok <token> <expert>:<slot> ... res <residual>` with its places in want-list rank order, or `tok <token> res <residual>` when it holds none. The residual is the sum of the gate scores it has at the experts of its want list, as that list finally stands, that it does not hold. Then comes `bal <n>`. There n adds up over the experts the number of tokens whose final want list holds that expert, multiplied by the number of places that expert kept. Nothing else is printed.
