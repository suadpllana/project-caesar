`/app` is the accounting half of a store that keeps file data in extents. A volume holds files. A file is an array of slots, and a slot is either empty or points at one block of one extent. An extent is a run of blocks allocated in one go, and its size never changes. A program is a text file of ops; `/app/progs` holds four of them, and `/app/run_st.py` takes one and prints a line for each thing that happens.

The ops are these. `vol v` makes an empty volume, and `fil v f n` makes a file of `n` empty slots in it. A write, `wr v f lo hi`, allocates one extent with a block for each slot from `lo` to `hi` and points those slots at its blocks in order, dropping whatever they held. A copy, `cp v f lo hi w g off`, takes the pointers of slots `lo` to `hi` of `v/f` and puts them in the slots of `w/g` from `off` onward; it reads them as they stood before the copy started, and where the source slot is empty the slot it lands in is emptied too. A trim, `tr v f lo hi`, empties the named slots. A snapshot, `sn v w`, makes a volume `w` holding, for every file of `v`, a file of the same name with the same pointers. A drop, `rm v`, removes a volume and every pointer in it. The import shorthand, `bulk v f n w`, makes file `f` with `n * w` slots and writes `n` extents of `w` blocks, the i-th covering the i-th run of `w` consecutive slots; nothing at all is printed for it. Four questions can be asked: `use v` for how much a volume is on, `own v` for how much smaller the store would be without it, `tot` for how much the store is holding, and `at v f i` for what one slot points at.

An extent takes up its whole size for as long as any block of it is pointed at, whatever slots of whatever volumes those are. It is given up the moment no slot anywhere points into it. A block is pointed at or it is not. Two slots on one block are one block and not two. A volume is on an extent while any slot of that volume points into it. What it is charged is the whole size of every extent it is on, each counted once however many of its slots are inside.

After the pointer changes of an op, and not partway through them, the store settles. First every extent no slot points into is given up. Then every extent that slots of exactly one volume point into, and that has twice its pointed-at blocks fewer than its size, is rewritten: a new extent is allocated with exactly as many blocks as were pointed at, those blocks keep the order they were in, every pointer into the old extent moves to the block of the new one that its own block became, and the old extent is dropped as part of the rewrite. Extents are numbered from 1 in the order they are allocated. A write takes its number before it touches a slot, and each rewrite takes one as it runs.

`use v` prints what `v` is charged. `tot` prints the total size of the extents the store is still holding. `own v` prints how much smaller that total would be if `rm v` ran next. `at v f i` prints what slot `i` of file `f` of volume `v` points at.

Lines are printed as they happen. A write prints `put <extent> <blocks>`, an extent given up because nothing points into it prints `gone <extent>`, a rewrite prints `pack <old> <new> <blocks>` and nothing else for the extent it replaced, and the questions print `use <volume> <blocks>`, `own <volume> <blocks>`, `tot <blocks>` and `at <volume> <file> <slot> <extent> <block>`, with `none` standing in for the extent and block when the slot points at nothing. Within one op the write's line comes first, then a line for each extent given up in increasing extent number, then the rewrites in increasing number of the extent being rewritten. Nothing else is printed.

Two of the shipped programs are large. `/app/progs/wide.txt` imports sixty thousand extents of four blocks, shares a thousand of them with a second volume, and then thins them one extent at a time, asking after each. `/app/progs/deep.txt` imports thirty thousand extents of eight blocks, snapshots them, thins five thousand from both sides and drops the snapshot, which leaves five thousand extents to rewrite in a single op. The graded set is three programs of each of those two shapes and three hundred and forty-two smaller ones, and the whole of it has to run inside 60 seconds in the 2048 MB the container is given.
