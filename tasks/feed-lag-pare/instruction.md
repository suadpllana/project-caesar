`/app` is the half of a change log that decides what it may throw away while readers are still attached to it. A program file gives it a stream of commands, one per line. `/app/run_log.py` takes one and prints a line for each thing that happens. `/app/progs` holds four programs.

`set <k> <v>`, `add <k> <n>` and `del <k>` each append an entry for key `<k>`, and keys are non-negative integers. Entries take the sequence numbers 1, 2, 3 and so on in the order they appear, and the head is the sequence number of the last entry, or 0 before the first one. `mark <m>` places mark `<m>` at the head and `unmark <m>` takes it away. `feed <f> <a> <b>` opens feed `<f>` at the head over the keys `<a>` to `<b>` inclusive, `ack <f> <s>` moves its position, and `close <f>` closes it. `read <p> <k>` reads key `<k>` at the point of mark or feed `<p>`. `pare <n>` shrinks the log. A name is used once in a program and taken away at most once, and a read names a mark, or a feed together with a key that feed covers.

The files you may change are `/app/lg/store.py`, `/app/lg/pin.py`, `/app/lg/fold.py`, `/app/lg/span.py`, `/app/lg/pare.py` and `/app/lg/tell.py`. Nothing else. The rest of the tree is replaced by our own copy before a program is run, a new file put beside those six included, so the driver that calls into them is ours and the six have to go on working with it.

The value of a key at a point is got by applying, in sequence order, every entry of that key the log still holds at or below the point, starting from absent. `set` makes the value what it names. `add` raises the value by what it names, and makes an absent key present at that amount. `del` makes the key absent.

A key is held at the head, at the point of every live mark, and at the position of every live feed whose range covers it. Two pins on one point are one held point. The trailing point of a key is the lowest position among the live feeds that cover it, and the head when no live feed covers it. An entry above its key's trailing point is never removed and never rewritten. Those of its held points standing at or below the trailing point cut the rest of the key into spans, taken in ascending order: the bottom of the log up to the first of them, that one up to the next, and so on as far as the trailing point. A held point belongs to the span it tops, so an entry standing on one is inside the span below it and not the span above.

`ack <f> <s>` moves feed `<f>`'s position to `<s>` when `<s>` stands above the feed's current position and not above the head, and leaves the position where it is otherwise.

Collapsing the span of one key removes the entries of that key the log holds inside it. One entry is left behind when the key has different values at the top of the span and at its floor, absent counting as equal to absent. That entry sits at the sequence number of the last entry of that key the log still holds inside the span, and it becomes `set <k> <v>` carrying the value at the top of the span, or `del <k>` when the key is absent there. A key and one of its spans therefore stand to remove the number of entries of that key the log holds inside it, one less when an entry is left behind. `pare <n>` works while the log holds more than `n` entries and at least one such pair stands to remove an entry: it collapses the pair standing to remove the most, a tie going to the pair whose span has the lower top and then to the smaller key.

A read prints `val <p> <k> <v>`, with `-` in place of the value when that key is absent at that point. A pare prints `pare <removed> <kept>`, the entries that command removed and the entries the log holds afterwards. When the program ends the log prints `log <kept>` with the number of entries it is left holding. Then comes one line for each key still holding an entry, in ascending key order: `k <key> <item> <item> ...`, one item per entry of that key in sequence order, `<seq>s<v>` for a `set`, `<seq>a<n>` for an `add` and `<seq>d` for a `del`. Nothing else is printed.
