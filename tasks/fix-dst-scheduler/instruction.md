The job runner in `/app` decides which recurring jobs run on our single worker, and it has been wrong since we started running sites on more than one offset. `/app/run_plan.py` takes a plan file and prints one line per worker event. `/app/sked/zt.py`, `/app/sked/due.py`, `/app/sked/gate.py` and `/app/sked/lane.py` are the four files you may change. `/app/sked/read.py`, `/app/sked/rec.py`, `/app/sked/check.py`, `/app/sked/emit.py` and `/app/run_plan.py` stay exactly as they are, nothing outside those four files is read, and `sked.lane.run(plan)` still has to hand back a list of `sked.rec.Ev` records for `sked.emit.lines` to print.

Run `/app/run_plan.py /app/plans/coast.txt`. It prints `start sweep 1 1575` on the second morning. The `coast` zone puts its clock forward an hour at minute 1560, so the local minute that job is due at never happens that day, and 1575 is an hour later than the first minute the site's clock actually reaches. `/app/plans/island.txt` is a second plan. Both are ordinary.

Times are whole minutes. An instant is minutes from the plan's epoch; a local minute is the instant plus the zone's offset at that instant. A `zone` line gives a name and a base offset, and each `shift` line gives an instant and the offset in force from that instant on. A `pool` line gives a name, a zone and a cap. A `job` line gives an id, a zone, a priority, a pool, a duration, an opening and a closing time of day, a mode, a step and an anchor. A `horizon` line gives the length of the plan. The time of day of an instant is its local minute modulo 1440, and the local day of an instant is its local minute divided by 1440, rounded down.

One local minute can name no instant or two. Where instants exist whose local minute is exactly the one wanted, take the latest of them; where none does, take the first instant whose local minute is past the one wanted.

A job in `clock` mode has its occurrence `k` due at the local minute `anchor + k * step`, resolved to an instant by the rule above, so a day on which the clock moves is a day that is an hour shorter or longer than the step suggests. A job in `follow` mode has occurrence 0 due at the local minute `anchor`, resolved the same way, and every later occurrence due `step` minutes in elapsed time after the instant the one before it was attempted, with no local resolution at all. An occurrence is attempted when it starts, and also when it is dropped, at the minute it is dropped; an occurrence that is skipped is never attempted.

Admission goes by time of day: a job admits an instant whose time of day is at or after the opening and strictly before the closing. An occurrence due at an instant its job does not admit is dropped at that instant. Otherwise it may start at any instant from when it is due up to but not including the first instant after that which its job does not admit, and if it has not started by then it is dropped at that instant. A shift inside a window moves that boundary with it, so the minute a window closes is not always a fixed distance from the minute the occurrence was due.

An occurrence that comes due while its job still has an occurrence that has not ended, been dropped or been skipped is skipped at the minute it comes due, and the occurrences of a job are numbered from 0 in the order they come due, counting the ones that are skipped and the ones that are dropped.

The worker runs one job at a time and never interrupts a run that has started: a run holds the worker for its duration, so a job of any priority waits while a lower-priority run finishes. Whenever the worker is free, the waiting occurrence whose job has the smallest priority number starts, among those that have come due, have not started, been dropped or been skipped, and whose pool has cap left for that instant. Priorities are distinct. A waiting occurrence that cannot start because its pool is full does not keep the worker shut: the next eligible one takes it. Nothing is planned for an instant later than it could have started. A pool allows at most `cap` starts per local day of the pool's own zone, which is not always the local day of the job that is running, and the charge falls on the day the run started.

Within one instant, runs that finish do so first, then occurrences come due, then deadlines fall, then the worker starts at most one run. A run of duration zero is not possible, so no two runs ever start on the same minute.

Every event whose instant is below the horizon is printed and no event at or above it is, so a run that starts inside the horizon and would finish outside it prints its start and nothing else. A line is the kind, the job id, the occurrence number and the instant, separated by single spaces, where the kind is one of `end`, `skip`, `drop` and `start`. Lines come in order of instant, then in that order of kind, then by ascending priority. `/app/sked/emit.py` already prints them that way.

Plans keep to these bounds and `/app/sked/check.py` rejects the ones that do not: shifts are listed in increasing order of instant and no earlier than minute 1, caps and durations and steps are at least 1, a window opens at or after minute 0 and closes at or before minute 1440 and is strictly shorter than 1320 minutes wide, a `follow` job's step is longer than its duration, an anchor is at least as large as the biggest offset its zone ever takes, and the horizon is at least 1.

We grade every printed line of every plan, exactly, all or nothing, on the two plans in the tree and on several hundred plans you have not seen. The graded run has 600 seconds for all of them together.
