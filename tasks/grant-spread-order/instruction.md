The tree under `/app` is a small policy kernel we use to try access rules out without standing up a directory service. Nodes hang off one another, subjects are people and crews, a crew holds people and other crews, and an entry placed on a node says that one subject is allowed or refused one right. Every entry carries the subject it names, the right it governs, allow or refuse, its scope, the node it was placed on, and the sequence number of the act that placed it, and a journal is a list of administrative acts in a text file that the kernel walks one at a time. `/app/hist` holds a few. `/app/run_hist.py` takes one and prints what the kernel did with it: each act in order, a digest of the whole store after every one, each question and the answer it got together with the entry that carried the answer, and a dump at the end of what every node was left holding.

Scope is one of three. An entry governs the node it sits on and stops there, or it governs that node and everything under it, or it governs everything under it and leaves that node alone. Placing an entry sends it down the tree, so a node holds what was put on it directly alongside whatever reached it from above, and the dump at the end of a run shows the two together with the node each entry started from.

Some ground rules, several of which are not what you would do elsewhere.

A question names a subject, a node and a right, and it is answered from the entries standing on that node. Nothing walks the tree. Three things settle which entry stands, in this order, among the ones there that govern the right, that are not the kind which leaves its own node alone, and that name something the asker can reach. An entry naming the asker outright beats one naming a crew the asker belongs to, which beats one naming a crew that holds that crew, counting the shortest chain of memberships. An entry placed on that node then beats one that arrived from anywhere else. What is left goes to the later act. Allow and refuse never enter the ordering. A question nothing matches is a refusal. How far the asker stands from a subject is worked out when the question is asked, so a membership going up or coming down moves no entries anywhere.

Everything a node holds is either what was placed on it or what its parent offers, and we grade the holdings as hard as the answers. An entry of the kind that stops at its own node is offered to nobody, and an entry of the kind that leaves its own node alone reaches every node beneath it at every depth, whatever stands in the tree between the two. Clearing an entry takes it off the node it was placed on and takes the copies with it, everywhere that placement reached. An entry never returns to where it was placed.

A node can stop accepting inheritance, and what that stops is what arrives from above it: nothing granted or cleared higher up reaches it while the bar is up, its children go on agreeing with it and not with the chain over its head, an entry placed on it by hand still lands and still reaches those children, and it keeps everything it is holding wherever it is moved to, entries from nodes no longer above it included. When the bar comes down the node ends up holding what its parent offers alongside whatever was placed on it directly.

Structure moves under the kernel and a whole subtree answers for it: a node that moves takes its descendants with it, every one of them is under a different chain afterwards, and an entry that arrived from somewhere no longer above a node goes when that node moves, while a node made under a parent takes what that parent offers at once.

Four things have to keep working and we grade them as hard as the rest. A direct placement survives any structural change. A move that leaves a node under the same chain leaves its holdings alone. A node that is not accepting inheritance keeps every entry it had when the bar went up. An entry governing a node and everything under it answers at both.

We grade the answers and the holdings, in order, exactly, with no partial credit, over the journals in `/app/hist`, a harder set you have not seen, and several hundred generated ones besides, so a rule that happens to fit the acts in front of you will not carry. There is no expected output in your tree and none is coming. The kernel is the only thing that will tell you what your rules do.
