The tree under `/app` is a small cooperative runtime we use to try scheduling ideas out without standing up a real event loop. A program is a list of ops in a text file and `/app/progs` holds a few of them. Fibers take turns on a virtual clock, and everything that happens lands in a trace: which fiber came on, which token it produced, which guard opened and closed and how, which guard was marked and why, which mark was delivered to whom, which cleanup block began, which band opened and closed and what left it, and what each fiber was carrying when it ended. `/app/run_prog.py` takes a program file and prints that trace with every fiber's tokens.

A guard is a region a fiber can be pulled out of, carrying an optional deadline and a shield flag, and it becomes marked either by an op or by its deadline arriving. A band is a region that owns children: fibers spawned into it run alongside their parent, and the band cannot close until every one of them has ended. Cleanup blocks hang off guards.

Some ground rules, several of which are not what you would do elsewhere.

A fiber may not run one more op of its program inside a guard it can still see that has been marked, which means a mark reaching a fiber stops it where it stands and travels outward until it comes to rest at exactly one guard, and the fiber carries on from the far side of that guard only when nothing it can still see is marked at that moment. We grade both halves. Keeping going while something visible is marked is wrong, and stopping when nothing is marked is wrong too. Marks do not wait for a cut to finish travelling. A deadline can arrive, or another fiber can mark a guard, while a cut is already on its way out, and the programs we grade do that. A sleeping fiber is no different, and a mark that reaches one wakes it at the moment the mark lands, before its wait has run out.

Shields decide what a fiber can see. Scanning outward from the fiber, the first guard carrying a shield is the last one in view and everything enclosing that guard is hidden for as long as the shield stays up, though the shield never hides the mark on the guard carrying it; and because shields go up and down while a fiber sits inside the guard, and because marks are sticky and nothing anywhere clears one, a guard that was hidden comes back into view at the next checkpoint after the shield drops. A guard stops mattering when it closes. Not before. One consequence is worth stating on its own: the same guard reaches the same fiber more than once in a run.

A cleanup block belongs to the innermost open guard and runs when that guard closes, whichever way it closed, after it has closed, so its own checkpoints answer to whatever encloses it and never to the guard it came from. A mark that reaches a fiber inside a cleanup block abandons the rest of that block, and when such a block raises something of its own while an exception is already travelling, the newer one keeps going and the older one is gone.

Bands hold the rest of it. A fiber standing at the close of a band cannot leave while a child is alive, whatever has reached it, and it is asked again when the last child ends; a fiber that is already unwinding when it reaches a band it owns stops the children before it waits on them; and a child answers to the chain that stood when its band was opened, never to the guards its parent has opened since. The close itself has three outcomes. A mark on a guard enclosing the band outranks anything the children collected and leaves on its own; otherwise what the children collected leaves together, ordered by the tick each child ended on and then by the order the children were made; otherwise a mark on the band's own guard is what the close was for and nothing leaves at all. A child that ended carrying a mark contributes nothing, and a child that ends any other way hands what it was carrying to the band and marks the band's own guard. That is what stops the siblings. Errors are not marks. No guard takes an error, marked or not.

Four things have to keep working and we grade them as hard as the rest. A fiber with nothing marked anywhere runs every op it has. A shielded region runs to its end with an enclosing guard marked the whole time it is inside. A deadline belonging to a guard a fiber is not inside leaves that fiber alone, asleep or awake. A child that shields itself against its band runs to its own end and keeps its tokens.

We grade the trace and the tokens, in order, exactly, with no partial credit, and we grade the programs in `/app/progs`, a harder set you have not seen, and several hundred generated ones besides, so a rule that happens to fit the cases in front of you will not carry. There is no expected output in your tree and none is coming. The runtime is the only thing that will tell you what your rules do.
