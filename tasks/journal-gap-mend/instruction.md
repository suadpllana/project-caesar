`/app` is the recovery tool for our lock service's journal. `/app/mend.py` reads a journal file and prints, for every stretch of it a crash lost, the entries it can put back. Four journals to try it on are in `/app/journals`.

The service keeps locks 0 to L-1 for sessions 0 to S-1. A lock is either free or held by one session at a depth of at least 1, and it has a queue of waiting sessions, first come first served. The journal records every request with the outcome the service gave it. A request `acq l s` asks for lock l. A free lock is taken at depth 1 (`grant`), a lock the session already holds goes one deeper (`again`) and a lock someone else holds puts the session at the back of its queue (`wait`). A release, `rel l s`, is only ever sent by the holder and takes one off the depth. If the depth is still above 0 the outcome is `keep`. Otherwise the first session in the queue takes the lock at depth 1 (`pass`), or, with nobody queued, the lock becomes free (`free`). Last, `beat s` is a heartbeat, which a session sends only while it holds at least one lock, and it changes nothing. A session in a queue is waiting, and it sends nothing at all until a `pass` hands it the lock.

A journal's first line is `cfg L S K`. The service starts with every lock free and four counts at zero: grants (every `grant` and every `pass`), requests (every `acq`), releases (every `rel`) and heartbeats. Right after any entry that brings the grant count to a multiple of K, and at no other time, the service writes a digest, `dig <grants> <holders>`, with the count and the table as they stand after that entry. At moments of their own choosing an operator writes an audit, `aud <grants> <requests> <releases> <heartbeats> <whole>`, with the four counts and the table as they stand at that moment. The fingerprints `<holders>` and `<whole>` are what `holders` and `whole` in `/app/jl/fp.py` return for the table given as one `(holder, depth, queue)` row per lock in lock order, each queue running from its first waiter to its last and a free lock being `(None, 0, ())`: `holders` covers who holds each lock and nothing else, and `whole` adds depths and queues. The last line of every journal is an audit taken after its last entry. That entry survived.

Digests and audits are stored apart from the journal. All of them survived. Where entries were lost, the file has a line `gap`, then every digest a lost entry triggered and every audit taken between the entry before the loss and the entry after it, in the order they were written, then a line `back`. A lost stretch may have held any number of entries, none included. Nothing says where among its lost entries an audit listed inside it was taken.

An account of a journal fills every lost stretch with entries so that, replaying the whole journal from the start, every entry is one the service would accept, with the outcome shown, and every digest and audit is exactly what would have been written where it stands, with no entry triggering a digest the file does not have. The journal happened, so it has at least one account. For each lost stretch, in order, print `gap <n>` with n counted from 1, then, one per line and exactly as the journal writes them, the entries that open that stretch in every account, for as long as all accounts agree. When they agree on the whole stretch, that is all. Otherwise print one more line: `? ` followed by every entry some account has at the first place they differ, and `-` if some account's stretch ends there, joined by ` | ` and sorted as plain ASCII text, so `-` comes first.
