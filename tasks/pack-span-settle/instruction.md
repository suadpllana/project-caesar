`/app` is the packing half of our pretraining data path, cut down to the part that decides what goes into a training window and what each position of one is worth. A shard is a text file of ops and `/app/shards` holds four of them. `width w` sets the width of the windows opened after it, `span n` how many windows a step holds, and `floor f` how many scored positions a step needs to be trained on. `rec id n w` declares a record of `n` tokens carrying weight `w`. Every shard ends with `seal`. `/app/run_shard.py` takes a shard and prints a line for each record and each step as it settles.

The second line comes out `lay b 1 2 5 3/5` and it should read `lay b 1 2 4 3/4`, since b was cut in two and a record loses a scored position at every cut. The files you may change are `/app/pipe/cut.py`, `/app/pipe/win.py`, `/app/pipe/lay.py`, `/app/pipe/step.py`, `/app/pipe/hold.py` and `/app/pipe/weigh.py`. Nothing else.

Records are laid in the order the shard gives them, into the window that is open and the windows after it. Windows are numbered from 1 in the order they are opened and steps from 1, in each shard. A window keeps the width it was opened with. A step keeps the span and the floor that were in force when it was opened. An op arriving while either is still open does not reach it. The piece a record puts in the open window is the longest run of at least two tokens that fits in the room left and leaves the record either finished or with at least two tokens still to place. When no such run fits, that window is closed with the room left in it unused. A window is also closed when it is full, and at `seal`. A record one token long has no position that could be scored, so it is passed over: it takes no room at all and prints `skip <id>` as it is read.

A position is scored when the position after it lies in the same window and belongs to the same record, so a piece of `t` tokens carries `t - 1` scored positions and a record of `n` tokens cut into `p` pieces carries `n - p` of them. A step holds `span` windows. It is trained on only if it carries at least `floor` scored positions. One below that is dropped, and prints `drop <step> <windows> <positions>` when it closes. A record's weight is spread evenly over the scored positions it has inside steps that were kept, so each of those positions carries the weight over that count, and a record left with none of them prints `void <id>` instead.

A record settles when the last step it has a piece in closes, which can be long after its last piece was placed, and a kept step settles once it has closed and every record with a piece in it has settled, so that when a step closes its `drop` line comes first if it was dropped, then the records that settle with it in the order the shard declared them, then the step lines they release in step order. A record prints `lay <id> <first window> <pieces> <scored> <num>/<den>`, where the first window is the one holding its first piece, `pieces` is how many it was cut into, `scored` is `n - p`, and the fraction is its weight over its divisor in lowest terms. A kept step prints `step <step> <windows> <positions> <num>/<den>`, where `windows` is how many it held, `positions` is the scored positions in it, and the fraction is the sum of what those positions carry, again in lowest terms. A fraction that comes out whole keeps its denominator, so a weight of three over one position is printed `3/1`. `seal` closes the window and the step still open. Nothing else is printed.

A shard gives a width of at least 4, a span and a floor of at least 1 before its first record, and records of at least one token carrying a weight of at least 1. No id appears twice. `/app/shards/wide.txt` lays forty-five thousand records and `/app/shards/deep.txt` twenty-five hundred, and each of them declares about four billion tokens.
