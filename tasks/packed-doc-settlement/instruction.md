The trainer in `/app` reports a different loss curve when we change the accumulation layout, and the curve it reports is wrong in ways that have nothing to do with the layout. `/app/train` is the trainer. Its runtime is `/app/train/feat.py`, `/app/train/feed.py`, `/app/train/lay.py`, `/app/train/box.py` and `/app/train/ops.py`, and those stay as they are. What a step does is decided by `/app/train/pick.py`, `/app/train/fold.py`, `/app/train/norm.py`, `/app/train/turn.py`, `/app/train/again.py` and `/app/train/keep.py`. Those six you may change. `/app/run_train.py` takes a run script and prints a line for every event in it.

Run `/app/run_train.py` on `/app/recipes/hold.txt`. Its second step prints `up 2 0.100000 0.000000 0.000000 0.332189 -0.442695 0.586095 0.117137`: an update, a loss of zero, and four parameters that have moved. That step read the first half of `cd`, which is marked as contributing nothing. It finished no document at all. It should have taken no step. `/app/recipes/mixed.txt` is a second script. Both are ordinary.

A script opens with settings and continues with events. The settings print nothing. `lim` is the number of tokens in a sequence. `bat` carries three numbers, the accumulation groups, the workers in a group and the sequences in a micro-batch, whose product is how many sequences a step wants. `opt` carries the base rate, the warmup length, the halving interval, the momentum factor and the clip. `ema` sets the ceiling on the averaging decay, `rep` the requeue threshold and the requeue allowance, and `par` the four starting parameters. The events are `doc`, with a name, a token count and a target index; `mdoc`, with a name and a token count, for a document that is read but contributes nothing; `step`; `resh`, with a fresh batch layout that holds from that point on; `save`; `load`; and `emit`.

Every event puts out a line of its own. A document gives `ad` and its name, a layout change gives `rs`, a checkpoint gives `ck` or `ld` with the number of steps taken so far, and `emit` gives `em` and the running average. A step gives either `nil` or `up` followed by the number of steps taken counting this one, the rate it used, its loss, the length of its gradient and the four parameters it left behind, and then one `rq` line naming each document it sends back for another pass. Numbers carry six digits after the point. Every line is graded.

A `doc` or `mdoc` line puts one document at the tail of the pending stream. A name declared twice is two documents. Token `i` of a document named `x` has the four features that `feat.vec(x, i)` returns, and the score of a token is those features against the current parameters. The stream is cut into sequences of exactly `lim` tokens in stream order, so a document runs on into the sequence behind it. A tail of fewer than `lim` tokens waits for more before any of it is read. A step reads the sequences that are ready, up to the number `bat` asks for, and fewer when fewer are ready. With none ready it reads nothing. How the runtime spreads what it read across micro-batches is a fact about where the work ran, and it must not move a single printed number.

A document settles in the step that read its last token, whatever earlier steps read the rest of it. A masked document is read and then dropped. It never settles, and it counts for nothing. The loss of a settled document is the logarithm of the summed exponentials of its token scores, less the score of its target token, and its gradient is the mean of its token features weighted by those exponentials, less the target token's features. Both are worked out once, when the document settles, over all of its tokens, against the parameters the settling step begins with. What the parameters were while the earlier tokens went past does not enter into it.

A step that settles nothing prints `nil` and leaves everything alone: no parameter, no momentum, no average, no position in the schedule, and no advance in the count of steps taken.

A step that settles something takes the plain mean of its settled documents, for the loss and for the gradient alike. The printed length is the euclidean length of that mean gradient before any shortening. A gradient longer than the clip is then scaled to exactly the clip, and the scaled gradient is the one momentum receives. Momentum becomes the momentum factor times itself plus that gradient. The parameters then lose the rate times the momentum.

The rate comes from the count of steps taken before this one. While that count is under the warmup, the rate is the base multiplied by one more than the count and divided by the warmup. From the warmup onwards it is the base halved once for every whole halving interval beyond it. The count then rises. The running average follows the parameter update: the new average is the decay times the old average, plus the new parameters weighted by one minus the decay, where the decay is one more than the risen count divided by ten more than it, or the ceiling from `ema` when that is smaller. Before any step is taken the average holds the starting parameters.

Documents settle in the order their last tokens stand in the stream, which need not be the order the micro-batches ran. Taken in that order, a settled document whose own loss is greater than the threshold goes back for another pass: a new document with the same name, length and target joins the tail of the pending stream behind everything already waiting there, and `rq` with the name is printed. The allowance in `rep` bounds how many times one document may come back. It is spent along the chain of returns, not against the name, so a document the script declares a second time begins with the whole allowance again. A document at or below the threshold stays put. The mean decides nothing.

`save` records the run. `load` puts it back as it stood, so what the steps after a `load` print is what the steps after the `save` printed. That takes the parameters, the momentum, the running average, the count of steps taken, the pending stream, how far into each unfinished document the run has read, and how much of its allowance each waiting document has already spent. The batch layout is not part of it. A `resh` between the two stays in force across the `load`. A `load` before any `save` does nothing. Whatever `keep.py` hands over is written out and read back as JSON, so it has to be built from numbers, strings, lists and dictionaries.

A graded script declares at most twenty documents of at most sixty-four tokens each, sets `lim` between three and twelve and each of the three batch numbers between one and four, and runs a few dozen events. Documents that outlast a step, steps that finish nothing, layouts that change under a running trainer, names that come round a second time, and checkpoints taken over a half-read document all occur in them. None of it is rare. We compare every printed line of every script exactly, on the scripts in the tree and on scripts you have not seen. Nothing outside the six files is read.
