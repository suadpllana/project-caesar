`/app` is the read path of a list service. It is cut down to the part that decides which rows go into the next page a client is sent. A list file gives a table of rows, the scrolls reading it, the pages they ask for and the edits that land between those pages. `/app/run_lst.py` takes one and prints a line for each page.

`cfg H` opens the file and sets the hold. `row <id> <k> <g> <w>` is a row present before anything runs, with sort key k, tag g and weight w. `open <s> <g> <n> <c>` opens scroll s over tag g, its pages carrying at most n rows and at most c weight. `next <s>` serves scroll s a page. The edits are `add <id> <k> <g> <w>`, `move <id> <k>`, `tag <id> <g>` and `drop <id>`. Keys and tags are integers. A weight is an integer and at least one. Row ids are unique over the whole file and are never reused, including after a drop. `/app/lists` holds four list files.

The files you may change are `/app/lst/seq.py`, `/app/lst/scr.py`, `/app/lst/owe.py`, `/app/lst/pg.py`, `/app/lst/edt.py` and `/app/lst/rep.py`. Nothing else. The rest of the tree is replaced by our own copy before a list file is run, a new file put beside those six included.

The place of a row is its key and then its id: a smaller key comes first, and among equal keys a smaller id. A scroll reads the rows carrying its tag as they stand at the moment it looks, and the order it reads them in is that place order. The mark of a scroll is a place. A scroll opens with its mark before every place.

A page carries at most n rows and at most c weight, and both are shared by the two halves of serving it. The first half drains the ledger of that scroll, strictly from the front: while the page has room and weight left, the row at the front goes out if its weight is at most the weight left, and otherwise the draining stops there. The second half is a scan over the rows of the view whose place is after the mark, in place order. A row this scroll has already been handed is passed by. A row whose weight is at most the weight left is handed out. Any other row is stepped over and the scan carries on. Throughout both halves, a page that has handed out nothing at all takes the row it would otherwise leave behind, whatever it weighs and whatever the hold stands at, and the weight of the page is spent to nothing by doing so. The mark moves to the place of every row the scan looks at, whichever of those things happened to it. Draining the ledger never moves the mark. The scan stops when the page holds n rows, when its weight is spent, when the rows it has stepped over in this page weigh c or more, or when the view has no further row.

A row is owed to a scroll when it carries the tag of that scroll, its place is at or before the mark of that scroll, and that scroll has not been handed it. The ledger holds the rows owed to a scroll in the order they came to be owed. A row that stops being owed leaves the ledger. A row that comes to be owed again goes in at the end.

The service holds at most H weight of owed rows across every scroll at once. A scan steps over a row only while the weight already owed plus the weight of that row is at most H. Where there is no room for it the scan stops there, and the mark does not move past that row. That hold governs what a scan may step over and nothing else. An edit that brings a row to be owed is not held back by it.

A row handed out to a scroll is never handed to that scroll again, whatever becomes of it afterwards. A row handed to one scroll is still handed to another scroll reading the same tag.

`pg <s>` is printed for each `next`, followed by the ids the page handed out in the order they went out, and alone when the page handed out nothing. Once the file ends, every scroll prints `sc <s> <d> <o> <u>` in ascending scroll number: d rows handed out to it over the whole run, o rows owed to it now, u rows of its view now that it has not been handed. Then comes `tot <w>`, the weight of the rows owed across every scroll. Nothing else is printed.
