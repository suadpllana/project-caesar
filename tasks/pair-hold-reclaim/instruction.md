The tree under `/app` is a small object store we use to try retention rules out without standing up a real one. A stream is a list of ops in a text file, `/app/streams` holds a few of them, and in one of them cells are made and linked to one another, held by name, entered into two tables; some are watched, and some carry a cleanup. A pass reclaims whatever the store is finished with, and everything a pass does lands in a ledger: which cleanup ran, which watch was emptied, which entry was dropped, which cell was let go, and what each read of a watch answered. `/app/run_ops.py` takes a stream and prints that ledger with the store it leaves behind.

A link is an ordinary reference from one cell to another. An entry is a conditional one, and the table is the only thing holding the value it names: a one-key entry names a key and a value, a two-key entry names two keys and a value. A watch names a cell without holding it. There are two kinds: plain and firm. A cleanup is a small action a cell carries, to be run once, when the store is finished with that cell.

Some ground rules, several of which are not what you would do elsewhere.

What a set of cells reaches is worked out the same way wherever that question comes up. A link puts the cell it points at in reach whenever the cell holding it is in reach. A one-key entry puts its value in reach for as long as its key is in reach, a two-key entry does so only while both of its keys are, and nothing else anywhere holds the value an entry names. What the store keeps is what the bound names reach. A cell the bound names cannot reach is let go. We grade both halves: letting go of something still in reach is wrong, and holding on to something nothing reaches is wrong too. When a pass is over, nothing in the store is out of reach.

A cleanup runs once in the life of a cell and never again, whatever becomes of that cell afterwards, and it runs with its cell and with whatever that cell can still reach in place, so a cell whose cleanup has not run is never let go. The action lands on the store where it runs. The names stay bound around it. Cleanups put things back and cleanups let things go: an action can bind a name or add a link or enter a pairing or read a watch, and it can unbind a name or cut a link, and the streams we grade do both of those things, which is why a cleanup may use what its cell reaches and therefore does not run while anything else with a cleanup still pending can reach that cell; where nothing still pending reaches them, all of them go together, oldest first; and where every one of them is reachable from another, the oldest goes ahead on its own. One shape is worth stating on its own, because it decides which of two cleanups is which: a two-key entry can have one of its keys held by a name and its other key inside the group being cleaned up, and the streams we grade do that.

A plain watch is emptied as soon as a pass finds its cell out of reach, and a firm watch is emptied only when its cell is let go. No watch of either kind names a cell after that cell has gone, so a cell is let go with whatever still names it emptied immediately beforehand, and watches are emptied oldest first. An emptied watch never fills again. Whatever becomes of the cell it named, the answer stays empty.

Cells are let go oldest first. An entry is dropped when either of the cells it names goes. An op that names a cell that is no longer there does nothing at all.

Four things have to keep working and we grade them as hard as the rest. A pass that finds nothing out of reach writes no rows. A value held through a chain of entries stays. A group of cells that can only reach one another goes, and its entries go with it. A cell its own cleanup puts back keeps its firm watch.

We grade the ledger and the store it leaves behind, in order, exactly, with no partial credit, and we grade the streams in `/app/streams`, and a harder set you have not seen, and several hundred generated ones besides, which together mean that a rule which happens to fit the cases in front of you will not carry.
