The tree under `/app` is a small recompute engine we use to try settling rules out without standing up a real one. A panel is a list of declarations and rounds in a text file and `/app/panels` holds a few of them. A feed is a value written from outside. A gauge is a value worked out from a small integer expression over other entries, and one of the expressions is a conditional, so which entries a gauge reads is settled while it runs. A latch hangs off an entry and reports what that entry came to rest at, and it may write feeds of its own. `/app/run_panel.py` takes a panel and prints what the engine did with it: every feed a round moved, every gauge it ran and the value that gauge took, every latch that tripped and the value it saw, and what every entry was left holding at the end.

A round is one settling. The writes land on their feeds, the engine works through whatever those moves reach, and the round is over when nothing is left to run. The panel comes up in a round of its own before any of the written ones. Every gauge takes its first value there. That round is graded like the rest.

Some ground rules, several of which are not what you would do elsewhere.

A gauge takes a value once in a round and never twice, it never takes one the same round then moves again, and it takes one only when something it reads has moved. We grade all three. Running a gauge the round is going to overtake is wrong, running it a second time is wrong, and leaving it alone when something it reads has moved is wrong as well. Distance from the feeds decides which gauge goes next. A feed stands at nothing. A gauge stands one step beyond the deepest entry it reads, the nearest of them goes first, and among gauges standing equally far the one the panel declares earliest goes first. No two gauges tie. Declaration order is not wiring order, and the panels we grade make a point of that.

What a gauge reads is not a property of the panel, because a conditional takes one arm or the other according to a value, so a gauge can start reading an entry it was not reading before, and stop reading one it was, and the panels we grade do both of those while a round is still settling. Arms swap under load.

A latch reports what its entry came to rest at, so latches are asked once the round has gone quiet, and never before then. Every latch whose entry moved during that round trips, once each, in the order the panel declares them, and each one reports the value its entry was left holding. What the latches that tripped write is the next round, taken in the order they tripped, and where two of them write the same feed the later one stands. A round made this way is a round like any other and can trip latches again. Nothing trips while the panel is coming up.

Four things have to keep working and we grade them as hard as the rest. A feed written the value it already holds has not moved and wakes nothing, a gauge that runs and comes back with the value it already had has not moved either so a latch standing on it does not trip, a panel with no conditional anywhere in it settles once per round and is never asked for anything more, and a feed that nothing reads moves without waking a thing.

No panel we grade needs a gauge to take a value twice in one round, so nothing turns on how you would order that.

The arithmetic is integer throughout. The engine is deterministic, and two runs over one panel agree exactly. We grade what the engine did and what it was left holding, in order, exactly, with no partial credit, over the panels in `/app/panels`, a harder set you have not seen, and several hundred generated ones besides, built after you have stopped, so a rule that happens to fit the panels in front of you will not carry. There is no expected output in your tree and none is coming. The engine is the only thing that will tell you what your rules do.
