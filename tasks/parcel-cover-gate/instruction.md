The tree under `/app` is a small settings fabric we use to try replication rules out on without standing up a real one. Workers write settings, publish a picture of a named band of them, and hand those pictures to one another; each of them shows one version of every setting it has caught up with, every read it answers goes through what it shows, and being handed a picture is not the same as showing one. A feed is a list of ops in a text file. `/app/feeds` holds two. `/app/run_feed.py` takes one and prints what each read answered, what each worker came to show as it went, and the picture every one of them is left holding at the end.

Some ground rules, and several of them are not what you would do elsewhere.

Nothing shown may rest on a picture its holder has not caught up with; every version carries the picture its writer was displaying at the instant it wrote, one line per setting that writer had, and a worker has caught up with such a line when what it shows of the setting is either the version named on it or a version that was itself written standing on the one named there. Nobody goes backwards, either. The versions of a setting a worker comes to display run one behind the next, each written standing on the one it replaces. Two of them showing the same version can both write it, and of the pair that comes out neither stands after the other, so whoever holds one will never hold the other. That is no rarity in what we grade.

A parcel goes up whole or it does not go up. There is no half of one. Afterwards its recipient displays, for every setting named inside, the version named there or something standing after it. Nothing handed over is discarded while it could still go up, and whatever can go up does, at the moment it can, whatever brought that about. One parcel rising is among the things that can free another. It can equally be what blocks another for good, so where more than one thing in hand could rise at the same instant, the earliest to arrive is the one that does. Waiting is not failing.

Settling a setting stands on both the version its holder displays and the version the parcel carries, and takes the value of whichever was written later. It is a write. Whoever settles is the writer. So the picture behind a settled version is theirs at that instant, and the picture behind the version taken in does not travel across with it.

A read answers with the value of the version its worker displays: the number where there is one, `x` where the setting was withdrawn, and `-` where nobody there has ever heard of it. Four further things have to keep working and we grade them as hard as the rest. A parcel with nothing left to contribute writes no row. Anyone handed a parcel they published themselves is unmoved by it. A line naming a setting its recipient has never heard of is a line nobody there has caught up with. And whatever a worker has come to display, it goes on displaying, however much arrives afterwards.

Our feeds are untidy on purpose. Parcels arrive in an order bearing no relation to the order they were published, a picture can rest on a setting no band its recipient ever receives mentions, and a band somebody is already past in part and behind on in the rest turns up constantly. Settling frees pictures that could not rise an op earlier. Settings get rewritten while a picture of the older one is still queued. All of it is graded.

We grade every read a feed asks for and every settling of a shown map, in the order the ops produced them, exactly, with no partial credit, together with the picture each worker is left holding at the close; and we grade the feeds in `/app/feeds`, a harder named set you have not seen, and several hundred generated ones besides, which together mean that a rule which happens to fit the two runs in front of you will not carry, and that anyone left displaying less than they were entitled to costs the whole feed as surely as a wrong value does. No expected output sits anywhere in your tree and none is coming. The fabric is the only thing that will tell you what your rules do.
