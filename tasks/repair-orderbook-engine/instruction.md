`/app` is the matching engine we try rule changes on before they go near the live one. A session is a text file of messages and `/app/sess` holds a few. `cap` says how far from the last trade a fill may happen, and `mark` says what that price is before anything has traded. A session may select `pace order` or `pace fill` once, before its first `new` or `pull`; omission means `order`. This selector prints nothing. `new` carries an id, a participant, a side, a price, a size, a shown size, a time in force and a trip price, with `-` wherever there is none. Order ids are unique within a session and need not increase. `pull` carries an id. `/app/run_book.py` takes a session and prints what the engine did with it, a line per event, then the book it ended on.

Some ground rules, and several of them are not what you would do elsewhere.

An arriving order takes the other side best price first, and at a price in the order the resting orders stand there. A fill happens at the resting order's price. A buy never fills above its own price and a sell never below it; an order with no price of its own carries no such bound. No fill may happen further than `cap` from the last trade price. That price is whatever the most recent fill made it: it starts at `mark`, and nothing else moves it. The walk stops at the first resting price it may not trade at. It does not reach past one to a price further along.

A resting order with a shown size offers only that much. When the shown part is used up the order discloses the next one, its shown size or what is left of it, whichever is smaller, and goes to the back of the orders standing at its price. That is a `shw` line every time it happens. An order with no shown size offers everything it has.

An arriving order never trades with a resting order carrying its own participant. That order comes off the book instead. All of it, the part it was not showing included, with no fill and a `pul` of `same`, and the walk carries on with whatever now stands in front of it.

A `part` order fills what it can and the rest is cancelled. An order with no price of its own cannot rest. A `day` order with a price rests what is left of it at that price, behind everything already standing there, showing its shown size or the remainder, whichever is smaller.

`whole` is the awkward one. It must receive its entire size during its execution and never rests, and it finds out whether it is short by walking: a fill it makes on the way is a fill for the parked orders like any other. If it comes up short, put everything back the way it stood at the point it began, with one exception. Discard every effect and printed line from its execution, including work done by orders it ran, and print `pul <id> whole`. The exception is a firing. It is not taken back. A parked order that fired on a fill inside that execution, at any depth, has left the parked set for good, and those orders form one batch of their own, in arrival order like any batch: after the cancellation line comes a `trp` line for each of them, and then they execute, from their full size, against the restored book and last price. With `pace order` that batch joins the end of the waiting queue; with `pace fill` it runs before anything that was waiting on the failed order. A fired order that fails as a `whole` itself stays fired. If an enclosing `whole` later fails, its cancellation line goes with the rest of the discarded output and it executes again after the enclosing order's cancellation, with everything else that fired inside it. A no-price `whole` uses the same cancellation reason; other no-price remainders use `mkt`, and priced `part` remainders use `part`.

An order carrying a trip price is parked off the book. Nothing fires a parked order except a fill, and any fill can fire any of them: a parked buy fires when the last trade price is at or above its trip price, a parked sell when it is at or below. A firing removes the order from the parked set; when executed it goes through the book as an arriving order without parking again. Orders firing on the same fill form a batch in their original arrival order, not by trip price or id. `pull` takes a resting or a parked order off with a `pul` of `user`, and an id that names neither prints nothing.

With `pace order`, fired orders join the end of the waiting queue and execute after the current order finishes. With `pace fill`, finish the fill and the resting order's removal or disclosure, then execute that fill's batch before continuing the interrupted order. Apply this rule inside a fired order too: the batch it fires finishes before the next member of the earlier batch runs. A batch caused by an order's last fill still runs before that order finishes. The interrupted order resumes against the book and last price left by this work. Fill-pace sessions have at most 24 parked orders waiting at once.

Every fill prints `trd <arriving> <resting> <price> <size>`. A cancellation prints `pul <id>` and its reason. A fully filled order has no cancellation line. A disclosure prints `shw <id> <shown>`, an order joining the book prints `rst <id> <price> <shown>`, a parked order prints `arm <id>`, a fired one `trp <id>`. If one fill both fires parked orders and exhausts a shown slice, its `trp` lines precede its `shw` line. When the session is done the engine prints the book it ended on: `bk b <price> <id> <shown> <remaining>` for the buys, best price first and in queue order within a price, then the same for the sells, then `am <id>` for every order still parked, by id. Nothing else is printed.

Two of the sessions in `/app/sess` are the big ones, and they are big in different ways. `wide.txt` is four thousand messages, nearly all of them orders parked off a book that stays small, with a handful of deeply sliced resting orders answering most of the fills between them. `book.txt` is the other shape. A hundred and eighty price levels holding three thousand orders that mostly never come into reach, and five hundred and sixty all-or-nothing orders arriving at the touch. The ones we grade run about three times larger than either. `/app/sess/fill.txt` is a small fill-pace input; graded fill-pace books also reach 9,200 initial orders and 1,700 whole-order episodes among subsequent messages. Whatever you write has to get through the whole set inside three hundred seconds.
