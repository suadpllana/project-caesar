`/app` is the layout half of a virtualized list pane: the part that decides where the pane scrolls to after each event and which rows it renders. A document is a run of groups, each a header of a declared height followed by rows whose real height the pane learns only by rendering them, and the pane can keep only so many of the heights it has learned. `/app/run_pane.py` takes an event file and prints a line for each frame. `/app/evs` holds five event files.

The first line of an event file is `cfg V K P E C`: the viewport height, the overscan, the pass cap, the estimate and the memory. Then come one or more `g <id> <header> <low> <high> <rows>` lines, the groups in the order they stand in the document, with distinct ids. The rest are events, one frame each, in order. `scroll <d>` adds d to the offset, `go <p>` sets it to p and `size <v>` sets the viewport height to v. `ins <group> <at> <n>` puts n new rows into that group at that row position, and `del <group> <at> <n>` takes n rows out of it from that position. Every number is an integer, every height, V, P, E and C is at least 1, K is at least 0, no group's low is above its high, a group may have no rows, and every edit position and count lies inside its group.

The flow is, group by group, the header and then its rows. Items are numbered from 0 across the flow and frames from 0. Row ids are handed out from 0 in the order rows are created: the rows of each `g` line in turn, then the rows of each `ins` as it is replayed. The real height of a row is `low + (id * 2654435761) % (high - low + 1)` over its group's bounds. A header is as tall as its group declares and a row the pane remembers is as tall as its real height. Any other row is as tall as the real height of the nearest row above it in the flow that the pane remembers, in whatever group that row sits, headers not counting. With no remembered row above it, it is E tall. The top of an item is the sum of the heights before it and the total is the sum of all of them. The foot is the total less V, or 0 when that is negative, and clamping holds an offset between 0 and the foot. The pane starts at offset 0 remembering nothing.

The pane remembers at most C rows. A row is remembered once it is measured. A deleted row is forgotten, and an inserted row is not remembered. When the pane measures a row while it already remembers C rows, it first forgets the one it has seen least recently. A row is seen by every pass whose window holds it, and of two rows last seen by the same pass the pane forgets the one nearer the start of the flow.

The pinned group is the last group whose header top is at or before the offset. The band is the smaller of its header height and the distance from the offset to the next group's header top, the total standing in for that top after the last group. The anchor line is at the offset plus the band. An item is visible when its top is less than the offset plus V and its bottom is greater than the offset, and the window runs from K items before the first visible item to K items after the last, clipped to the flow.

The frame holds the item whose top is at or before the anchor line and whose bottom is after it, or the last item when the line is on the total. It can only hold what it has laid out, so when that item is a row the pane does not remember, it holds instead the nearest item before it in the flow that is a header or a remembered row. The gap is the held item's top less the line.

A frame runs in this order. `scroll`, `go` and `size` apply their movement and the offset is clamped. An insert or a delete moves nothing. The pane is following the foot when the offset that movement leaves is the foot. The hold is taken next, on the document as it stands before any edit. Then the edit is applied. When it removes the held item, the hold becomes the first item that survives after it, or the last that survives before it when none follows, which may be a row the pane does not remember, and the gap moves by the top of the new item less the top of the old one, both as they stood before the edit. After an edit the offset is clamped again.

The frame then settles, in at most P passes. A pass works out the band and the window from the offset it begins with, and every row in that window counts as seen by it from that moment. It then goes through the window in item order and measures each row the pane does not remember at the moment it gets there. After that it puts the offset at the foot if the pane is following the foot, and otherwise at the held item's top less the gap less the band that pass worked out, clamped. A frame is settled once a pass has measured nothing and left the offset where it was, and it stops after P passes however unsettled it is.

Each frame prints `f <frame> s <offset> g <group> b <band> w <first> <last> h <item> <gap> m <rows> p <passes>`, starting with the offset the frame ended at. The pinned group's id, the band and the window's first and last items are all four as the last pass worked them out. The hold is as it stood after the edit (`H` and its group's id for a header, `R` and its row id for a row) and comes with its gap, and the line ends with the measurements the frame made and the passes it ran. After the last frame comes `end s <offset> t <total> m <rows>`, where the rows are every measurement of the run. A row measured again after being forgotten counts again. Nothing else is printed.
