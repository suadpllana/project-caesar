`/app` is the scan layer of a columnar store, cut down to the part that decides which pages get read. A segment file holds one table, the changes made to it since it was written, and the queries to run over it. `/app/run_scan.py` takes one and prints what happens. `seg G N K` opens the file with the statistics granularity, the row count and the column count. Rows and columns are numbered from 0. A column is stored as chunks and a chunk as pages, both in row order and together covering every row; chunks are numbered from 0 within their column and pages from 0 within their chunk, and the partitions of two columns need not line up. `ch C p s` opens a plain chunk of column C, and `ch C d s m e1 ... em` opens one with a dictionary of m entries, ascending; s is the sum of the non-null values its pages hold. Each `pg n u mn mx x f ...` line after it is a page of that chunk: n rows, u of them null, mn and mx the recorded low and high over the rest, x either `e` or `w`, and f either `v` followed by the n values or `i` followed by n indexes into the dictionary of the chunk, `-` for a null either way. Only a `d` chunk has `i` pages. `up C r v` says row r now holds v in column C, `-` for a null, and `del r` says row r was deleted; a row has at most one update per column. `qry` opens a query, `prd` carries one condition, `prj` names the columns to report and `end` closes it. A condition is `ge C v`, `le C v`, `eq C v` or `ne C v` against a value, or `nn C` and `nu C` for not-null and null. A null satisfies `nu` and nothing else. Every query names at least one condition and at least one column. `/app/segs` holds four segment files.

The rest of the tree is replaced by our own copy before a segment file is run, including any new file put beside those six. `/app/run_scan.py` calls `live.fresh` once per file and then `live.start`, `pick.run`, `live.rows` and `proj.run` for each query, so those five keep their names and the arguments they are handed.

Pages and chunks are never rewritten. A row's value in a column is its update when it has one, and what its page holds otherwise. A deleted row is never alive. Everything a page or a chunk says about itself describes it as written: its header, its sum and the counts a read of it yields. Deleted rows and replaced values are included.

A page header knows two things: u of its rows are null, and every other value lies between its bounds. Under `e` the bounds are the recorded pair. Under `w` that pair was rounded inward to a multiple of G, so the bounds are the recorded low less G-1 and the recorded high plus G-1. A page whose rows are all null writes both as `-` and has no bounds. What these prove about every row a page holds, its header settles: that a condition fails for all of them, or holds for all of them. Every non-null value on an `i` page is one of the entries of its chunk. So the dictionary, once consulted, settles a comparison for an `i` page the same way. No entry satisfying it fails every row; every entry satisfying it, with the page holding no null, passes every row. It says nothing about a `v` page and never settles `nn` or `nu`. How many entries a dictionary has, and which pages are `i`, is known without consulting it.

Reading a page prints `dc` with its column, chunk and page numbers, and yields its values; reading an `i` page does not consult the dictionary. Consulting the dictionary of a chunk prints `rd` with its column and chunk. A file is scanned with one memory. A page read or a dictionary consulted by any query is known to every query after it, and each prints only the first time. What is known of a row's value in a column is its update when it has one, and otherwise its page's header, the page's values once read, and for an `i` page its chunk's dictionary once consulted. A row dies the moment what is known shows that it fails a condition of the query. A condition is settled for a row once what is known shows whether the row satisfies it. A condition and a chunk of its column are a pending pair while a live row takes its value from one of the chunk's pages without the condition settled for it. Applying a pair takes the chunk's pages in order. For each page still holding such a row, a comparison on an `i` page whose dictionary is not yet consulted consults it first, however little it turns out to settle. Then, if such a row remains, the page is read.

Reading a page settles the exact count of every condition of the query over that column on that page, counted over the page as written. A page read before a query starts counts exactly from its start. Otherwise the count is the spread of the header. Take two widths. One is the width of the bounds, the high less the low plus one; the other is the width of the part the condition asks for. That part is the high less v plus one for `ge v`, v less the low plus one for `le v`, and one for `eq v` and `ne v` when v lies within the bounds and none when it does not. Where it comes out at nothing or less the count is zero for `ge v`, `le v` and `eq v`, and all of the non-null rows of the page for `ne v`. Otherwise cut the part down to the width of the bounds, multiply the non-null rows by it, divide by that width and round up; that is the count for `ge v`, `le v` and `eq v`, and for `ne v` it is the non-null rows less it. `nn` counts the non-null rows, `nu` counts the nulls, and a page with no bounds counts nothing for anything but `nu`. The count of a condition on a chunk is the sum of its counts on the pages of that chunk.

The pair applied next is always the one expected to leave the fewest rows alive. That is the smaller of the live rows that chunk holds and the count of the condition on it. A tie goes to the condition written earlier in the query, then to the lower chunk number. The conditions are done when nothing is pending.

Then comes `sel`, the number of live rows and a digest of them: start at zero and, for each live row in ascending order, multiply by 1000003, add the row number plus one and take the remainder by 2305843009213693951. After it every column the query names is reported, in the order it names them and once for each time it names it, as `prj`, the column, the count of live rows whose value there is not null, and the sum of those values. The rows a page supplies there are the live rows that take their value in that column from it. The report reads a page only for rows it supplies. It reads one only when the line could not be worked out without it, even if every other page it could read were read. To work it out there is what is known: the values of a page once read; that every non-null value of a page is its low, when its bounds are one value, or the entry, when it is an `i` page of a chunk whose dictionary has a single entry and is known; how many rows of each page are null; and the sum of each chunk. Before a chunk's reads the report consults its dictionary when that has a single entry, one of its `i` pages supplies a row, and knowing it would spare a read. It consults no other. Its reads come in chunk and page order. `qry` and the number of the query, counted from 0 within the file, opens each query, and nothing else is printed. Every query starts with every row not deleted alive.
