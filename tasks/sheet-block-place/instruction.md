Our calculation engine has been wrong since formulas stopped returning one number. `/app/sheet` is the engine. `/app/sheet/grid.py` holds the sheet and `/app/sheet/form.py` reads formulas; `/app/sheet/val.py`, `/app/sheet/see.py`, `/app/sheet/lay.py` and `/app/sheet/memo.py` decide what a cell is worth, what is standing in it and where a block of results goes, and those four are the files you may change. `/app/run_sheet.py` takes a script and prints one line per command: the command number, the command, then every cell that shows something, in address order.

Run `/app/run_sheet.py` on `/app/cases/plain.txt`. Line 2 puts a block of four numbers down column b. Line 3 asks for a block whose height is read out of `b2`, and the printout says `3 put d1 | a1=4 b1=1 d1=#blk b2=2 b3=3 b4=4`. `b2` is holding a 2, so `d1` should be a block of two rows. `/app/cases/mixed.txt` is a second script. Both are ordinary.

The sheet is ten columns, `a` to `j`, and twenty rows. An address is the column letter then the row number, so `c7`. Address order is by row first and then by column. A script has two commands: `put <address> <formula>` and `clr <address>`, and both print a line. A formula is numbers, addresses, ranges written `b3:d7`, `+`, `-` and `*` with the usual precedence, brackets, a leading minus, and the ten functions below. Scripts run to at most sixty commands.

A cell is worth a number, nothing at all, one of the three refusals `#ref`, `#blk` and `#cyc`, or a block: a rectangle of entries, each of them a number, a refusal or nothing. `SUM`, `MAX` and `CNT` take any number of arguments and read every entry of each. `SUM` adds the numbers and `MAX` takes the largest, both stepping over refusals; `MAX` with no number to take is `#ref`. `CNT` counts entries that are not empty, refusals included. All three ignore empty entries. `LEN` counts the cells its one argument covers, whatever is in them. `AT(x,k)` gives the k-th entry of `x` counting along each row in turn, exactly as it stands, so an empty one comes back empty; a `k` outside the range is `#ref`. `RUN(n)` is a column of `n` rows numbered 1 upwards. `REP(v,n)` is a column of `n` copies of `v` and `ROW(v,n)` is a row of `n` copies; `v` may be a refusal, in which case every entry is that refusal, but a block or a range is `#ref`. `KEEP(x,k)` is a column of the first `k` entries of `x`, and too few entries is `#ref`. `GROW(x)` keeps the shape it was given and adds one to every number in it, leaving empties empty and refusals as they are. Every count is a whole number, at least 1 and no more than the sheet has rows for a column of results or columns for a row of them; anything else is `#ref`, and a count that is itself a refusal comes straight back out.

A block belongs to the cell whose formula produced it. It fills that cell and the cells below and to the right of it. It is refused if the rectangle would leave the sheet, or if any cell of it other than the owner's own holds something, or if a block belonging to an earlier owner has already reached one of those cells. Earlier means earlier in address order. A refused owner prints `#blk` and fills nothing, so the room it would have taken is free for whatever comes after it, and a later block may stand where a refused one could not.

Reading a cell is therefore not reading its content. Go through the owners at or before it, meaning the ones no further down and no further right, in address order, and take the first whose block reaches it; the entry that lands there is what the cell is worth. Only if no block reaches it does the cell's own content answer, and a cell holding nothing reads as empty. An owner whose block is standing shows that block's first entry rather than anything about the block, and a formula that produces a range rather than a block shows `#ref`.

Working out a cell's value can therefore need that same cell's value again. When it does, the cell that was asked a second time reports `#cyc`, and so does every cell whose own value was being worked out on its behalf. That can take in a cell which had already finished computing something perfectly good.

Empty counts as zero in arithmetic but is still empty everywhere else. A block or a range used in arithmetic is `#ref`, and that is decided before anything else; otherwise a refusal on the left comes out ahead of one on the right. A `#cyc` anywhere in what a function was handed makes the whole call `#cyc`, and none of the functions steps over it the way they step over the other two.
