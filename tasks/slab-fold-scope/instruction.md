`/app` is the commit path of the partitioned store, cut down to the part that decides what a proposal does. A bucket holds slabs, a slab holds keys, and no two live slabs of a bucket hold the same key. A program is a text file of ops, and `/app/progs` holds four of them. `plan` opens a proposal against the head as it stands, `put`, `cut` and `fold` give it parts in the order they are to run, `push` runs it, `bulk p b n lo w g` is the import shorthand that opens `p`, gives it `n` put parts, the i-th covering `w` keys from `lo + i*(w+g)`, and pushes it, and of the two questions `rows b` asks how many keys a bucket holds while `at b k` asks which slab holds key `k`. A `put p b lo hi` names a range of keys to make live, a `cut` one to take out, a `fold` one to re-pack. A range names every key between its ends. `/app/run_tab.py` takes a program and prints a line for each thing that happens.

We rewrote this half last cycle and it has been wrong since. Run `/app/run_tab.py` on `/app/progs/tiny.txt`. The last line comes out `land b 2 0 20`, and it should read `land b 2 0 5`, since the cut covers twenty keys, five of them were live, and what a cut reports is what it took. The files you may change are `/app/tab/live.py`, `/app/tab/lay.py`, `/app/tab/wipe.py`, `/app/tab/mark.py`, `/app/tab/take.py` and `/app/tab/push.py`. Nothing else.

A push runs the parts in order, each against the bucket as the parts before it left it, and a put then makes every key of its range live in one slab of its own, every one of them arriving with this proposal, with any of those keys that were already live coming away from the slab that held them and into the new one, so that what the push reports as added is the count of keys in the range that were not live and what it reports as removed is what the cuts took, a cut taking every live key of its range out of the bucket.

A fold re-packs. It looks at the slabs all of whose keys lie inside its range, so one with a key outside does not qualify however much of it lies within, and among those it takes the ones holding only keys that were already there when the proposal was planned, leaving one that holds none of them where it stands. One holding some of each is the case that matters. The attempt is undone, the proposal is planned again at the head as it now stands and pushed once more, and only that second attempt stands. Fewer than two slabs to take and the fold does nothing at all. Two or more become a single slab, and re-packing changes nothing about when any of its keys arrived.

An undone attempt leaves nothing behind. Slabs are numbered from 1 in the order they are created, and an undone attempt gives its slab numbers back, so the second attempt starts numbering where the first one did. A proposal that creates no slab and removes no key is given no number of its own, and the head does not move.

Every line is printed when it happens. A proposal that lands prints `land <proposal> <number> <keys added> <keys removed>` and one that does nothing prints `void <proposal>`, while the questions print `rows <bucket> <keys>` and `at <bucket> <key> <slab>`, with `none` in place of the slab where no slab holds that key. Nothing else is printed.

`/app/progs/wide.txt` imports sixty thousand slabs of four hundred keys and pushes twenty thousand proposals at narrow ranges of them, and `/app/progs/deep.txt` imports forty thousand, re-packs the lot into one slab, and pushes twelve thousand more against it.
