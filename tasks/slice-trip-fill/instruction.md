`/app` is the matching engine we try rule changes on before they go near the live one. A session is a text file of messages and `/app/sess` holds a few. `cap` says how far from the last trade a fill may happen, and `mark` says what that price is before anything has traded. `new` carries an id, a participant, a side, a price, a size, a shown size, a time in force and a trip price, with `-` wherever there is none. `pull` carries an id. `/app/run_book.py` takes a session and prints what the engine did with it, a line per event, then the book it ended on.

Some ground rules, and several of them are not what you would do elsewhere.

An arriving order takes the other side best price first, and at a price in the order the resting orders stand there. A fill happens at the resting order's price. A buy never fills above its own price and a sell never below it; an order with no price of its own carries no such bound. No fill may happen further than `cap` from the last trade price. That price is whatever the most recent fill made it: it starts at `mark`, and nothing else moves it. The walk stops at the first resting price it may not trade at. It does not reach past one to a price further along.

A resting order with a shown size offers only that much. When the shown part is used up the order discloses the next one, its shown size or what is left of it, whichever is smaller, and goes to the back of the orders standing at its price. That is a `shw` line every time it happens. An order with no shown size offers everything it has.

An arriving order never trades with a resting order carrying its own participant. That order comes off the book instead. All of it, the part it was not showing included, with no fill and a `pul` of `same`, and the walk carries on with whatever now stands in front of it.

A `part` order fills what it can and the rest is cancelled. An order with no price of its own is cancelled the same way, whatever its time in force says. A `day` order with a price rests what is left of it at that price, behind everything already standing there, showing its shown size or the remainder, whichever is smaller.

`whole` is the awkward one. Such an order either fills its whole size when it arrives or does nothing at all, and nothing at all means the book is exactly as it was: nothing pulled, nothing re-shown, nothing fired, and no line printed for it but its own cancel. It never rests.

An order carrying a trip price is parked off the book. Nothing fires a parked order except a fill, and any fill can fire any of them: a parked buy fires when the last trade price is at or above its trip price, a parked sell when it is at or below. The ones that fire on the same fill are taken in the order they arrived, not by their trip prices, and each fires once and is not parked again. A fired order then goes through the book the way an arriving order would, in that order, once the order being worked on has finished with it. `pull` takes a resting or a parked order off with a `pul` of `user`, and an id that names neither prints nothing.

Every fill prints `trd <arriving> <resting> <price> <size>`. Every order that leaves without resting prints `pul <id>` and one of `same`, `part`, `mkt`, `whole` or `user`. A disclosure prints `shw <id> <shown>`, an order joining the book prints `rst <id> <price> <shown>`, a parked order prints `arm <id>`, a fired one `trp <id>`. When the session is done the engine prints the book it ended on: `bk b <price> <id> <shown> <remaining>` for the buys, best price first and in queue order within a price, then the same for the sells, then `am <id>` for every order still parked, by id. Nothing else is printed.

Two of the sessions in `/app/sess` are the big ones, and they are big in different ways. `wide.txt` is four thousand messages, nearly all of them orders parked off a book that stays small, with a handful of deeply sliced resting orders answering most of the fills between them. `book.txt` is the other shape. Three hundred and sixty price levels holding three thousand orders that mostly never come into reach, and five hundred and sixty all-or-nothing orders arriving at the touch. The ones we grade run about three times larger than either. Whatever you write has to get through the whole set inside a hundred and twenty seconds, so time it on both before you call it done.
