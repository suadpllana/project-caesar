`/app` is the allocation and accounting half of a copy-on-write block store, the copy we try changes on before they go near the shipped one. A program is a text file of commands. `/app/progs` holds a few, and `/app/run_store.py` takes one and prints a line per command.

Every program opens with `dev N`, the device size in blocks. After that, `n <line>` makes an empty line, `p <line> <new>` stamps it as a second line, and `d <line>` drops one. Lines hold items, addressed `<line>/<item>`. A write is `w <line>/<item> <off> <n>`, n blocks at that offset. A share is `s <line>/<item> <off> <n> <line>/<item> <at>`, which copies the coverage of that range onto the second item at `at`. Then `t <line>/<item> <n>` cuts an item back to n blocks, `x` removes one, and `v` lays one out again. The questions are `c <line>` for what a line is charged, `u <line>` for what a line and everything stamped from it is charged, `g <line>,<line>` for what dropping that set would give back, `f` for the state of the free map, and `m <line>/<item>` for the coverage of an item. A `#` starts a comment. Blank lines are skipped.

Blocks are handed out in spans. A request for n of them is met from the smallest free run that holds n, and from the lowest of those when two runs are the same size. When no single run holds n, the largest run is taken whole, again the lowest of those on a tie, and the rest of the request goes on from what is left; every piece taken that way is a span of its own. A request the free total cannot meet is refused, and leaves the store as it was. A span never splits and never grows. It goes back to the free map whole, and only once no claim covers any part of it, merging with whichever free runs touch it when it does.

A write settles its range one block at a time. Where the block is already covered, and the block of the span under it carries no claim but the one covering it, it is rewritten where it stands; every other block of the range is fresh, and so is every block past the end of the item. A span emptied by the claims the write replaces is in the free map before the fresh blocks are taken, and the write is refused only when the free total after that release is short of what the fresh stretches need. What comes back from the free map is laid over the fresh stretches in order of item offset. That is not one stretch to one span: a stretch can run off the end of one piece and into the next, and one piece can serve two stretches. An item grows to the end of the range it was written and no further.

A share copies coverage. It allocates nothing. The blocks it names in the source must lie inside the source item, whatever is covering them, and the destination range replaces whatever was there, so a share can be the last thing to leave a span. Both a write and a share can create the item they name. An item that does not exist yet can only be addressed at offset zero. Beyond that an offset may reach the end of an item but no further.

A line is charged for the whole length of every span it stands on, once however many claims it holds there, and that is its referenced space; the part of it that no other line stands on is exclusive space. Both numbers come back from `c`. A stamp gives the new line the same items covering the same blocks of the same spans, so both lines stand on everything the origin stood on. `g` takes one or more line names separated by commas. It reports the total length of the spans that no line outside that set stands on, which is what dropping all of them together would give back.

The family of a line is the line itself, every line stamped from it, and every line stamped from those in turn. `u` asks the two questions of the family: the spans any line of it stands on are its referenced space, and the spans no line outside it stands on are its exclusive space. A line made with `n` is stamped from nothing. When a line is dropped, the lines stamped from it count from then on as stamped from the line it came from, and from nothing when it came from nothing. A name made again after a drop is a new line: stamped from nothing, with nothing stamped from it.

`v <line>/<item>` drops the item's coverage and then takes as many blocks as the item is long, laying them over it from the start. It is refused, and changes nothing, when the free total the release would produce is short of the item's length.

Every command prints one line. `dev N` prints `dev N`, and `n a` prints `n a`. A stamp prints `p <new> items=K`, the number of items the new line was given. A drop prints `d <line> rel=R`, the blocks returned to the free map. A write prints `w <line>/<item> new=A keep=B rel=R`: the blocks taken, the blocks rewritten where they stood, and the blocks returned. A share prints `s <line>/<item> rel=R` and names its destination. Then `t` prints `t <line>/<item> rel=R`, `x` prints `x <line>/<item> rel=R`, and `v` prints `v <line>/<item> new=A rel=R`. A charge prints `c <line> ref=X excl=Y`, and the family question prints `u <line> ref=X excl=Y`. A `g a,b` prints `g a,b rel=R`, with the names exactly as the command wrote them. `f` prints `f free=T runs=K big=B`, the free total, the number of free runs and the longest of them, all zero when nothing is free. `m <line>/<item>` prints `m <line>/<item> size=N` and then the coverage as maximal stretches, one per space, each written `<item offset>:<span start>+<offset into the span>:<length>`. Nothing follows the size when the item is empty.

A refusal takes the place of the result. Same shape: the command letter, what it names, then `err=<code>`. The codes are `dup` for a name that already exists, `nosuch` for a line or item that does not, `gap` for an offset past the end of an item, `range` for a length below one or a range that leaves the source item, and `noroom` for a request the free map cannot meet. A share names its destination in a refusal as well.
